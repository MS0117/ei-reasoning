## Family: Implicit-PRM / token credit (63 papers)

### Scout synthesis

What the family says, mapped to our measurements. (1) Likelihood-contrast credit (implicit PRM / DPO-as-Q / adapter-vs-base / privileged PMI) is repeatedly reported to be diffuse or surface-driven: URIAL (shift on discourse markers), 'Why is your LM a poor implicit RM' (superficial token cues), IPVRM (token credits of implicit PRMs 'weakly identified'), iStar (token-level implicit rewards too noisy for long traces), Se-DPO (implicit reward lands on punctuation/line breaks), OREO (explicit value localizes errors better than the implicit log-ratio), and AntiSD (the privileged-teacher signal is PMI(y_t; gold) whose positive mass is connectives/verifiable claims and whose negative mass is deliberation tokens). Our M1/M3/M5/M6 are therefore replications, not anomalies; M4's token kinds and its first-third/last-third location pattern are independently confirmed by AntiSD, 'Rethinking OPSD' (privileged teacher penalizes reconsideration tokens) and PW-OPSD (teacher reliability rises with position, AUROC 0.83; local uncertainty only 0.57). (2) Where contrast signals are used successfully in 2025-26 they are used for MAGNITUDE, not sign: GCPO, DGPO, RLSD, OPPO and SDPG let the verifier set the direction and use contrast/entropy to reallocate or gate a sequence-level advantage; OPPO in particular accumulates the privileged per-token ratio into a prefix-level success belief rather than a local weight. (3) The signals with published evidence of localizing genuine decisions without rollouts are intrinsic to the student: base entropy / forking tokens (80/20, EAPO, ACPO, Sparse-but-Critical's causal swap test), excess-surprisal-over-entropy (TACO's tail score, ~2% of tokens), counterfactual token masking (OAR-P, 4x cost) and shared-prefix MC among siblings (TEMPO, lambda-GRPO-as-PRM, SPO-tree) — all cheaper than per-prefix MC and all self-contained. (4) Candidate ideas already published under other names: 'distill the base-with-gold teacher on verifier-filtered bridge trajectories for all-fail prompts' = HDPO (2603.23871; identical cliff definition, uniform per-token JSD, hybrid with GRPO); 'per-token KL to the base shown y* on student rollouts' = OPSD/SDPO; 'weight privileged signal by disagreement' = DemoPSD; 'ramp privileged weight by position' = PW-OPSD; 'gate privileged distillation to verifier-correct rollouts' = SDPG; 'condition the teacher on a successful sibling and credit the divergence point of failures' = HSD (2606.15576); 'sequence-level importance weight on filtered SFT data' = iw-SFT; 'down-weight high-surprisal tokens in SFT' = DFT/ASFT (and TACO/Lopti on the RL side); 'up-weight student-vs-reference excess loss tokens' = Rho-1; 'critical tokens via positive-vs-negative fine-tuned model contrast, penalize on rejected only' = cDPO; 'token-presence Fisher contrast across correct/incorrect siblings' = KTAE; 'first-error localization from probabilities across a batch of sibling trajectories' = Unsupervised PRMs. (5) Implications for our objective choice: (a) abandon g_t^+ / g'_t^+ as success-side token weights — the literature and M1-M4 agree they select style; (b) the survivable uses of our signals are sequence-level (C(y), adapter/base sequence log-ratio as an iw-SFT weight, OPPO-style prefix belief) or failure-side (negative privileged mass concentrated in the last third = wrong-turn detector, HSD-style divergence credit against the rescued sibling, cDPO-style penalty on rejected only); (c) a self-contained, rollout-free token weighting on rescued successes with published localization evidence would combine base entropy (forks) with a TACO-style excess-surprisal damping of class-B tokens and, per 'Rethinking OPSD', must not suppress reconsideration tokens; (d) because cliff examples are 1.6% of the mix, any per-token reweighting is numerically invisible unless the cliff examples are also up-weighted at the sequence level or trained in a separate phase (HDPO's lambda, SDPG's gated term); (e) the untested transfer question (set A -> held-out set B) is not addressed by any paper in this family — the closest, HDPO, reports only pass@k on the training distribution, so a transfer-measured study of how rescued trajectories are learned remains open.

### Papers

- **Free Process Rewards without Process Labels (Implicit PRM)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01981 (2024 (ICML 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Train an outcome reward model whose reward is parameterized as r_theta(y) = beta*log[pi_theta(y)/pi_ref(y)]; Proposition 3.1 shows this makes the per-prefix quantity q_t = sum_{i<=t} beta*log[pi_theta(y_i|y_<i)/pi_ref(y_i|y_<i)] an exact soft Q-value (beta*log E_ref exp(r/beta)), so token/step rewards are the increments of that log-ratio. Any outcome loss whose reward has this form works: DPO, CE on (x,y,label), NCA, KTO; CE on single unpaired responses is the most data-efficient. Signal used: outcome labels only; cost: one extra forward pass for reference and one for the ORM, 38x fewer FLOPs than Math-Shepherd-style MC labeling. Evidence of localization: best-of-64 reranking beats Math-Shepherd; no step-level benchmark or token visualization is reported.
  - relevance: Directly the 'likelihood-contrast = credit' idea. Our M5 test (stage-2 DPO adapter's log-ratio vs base does not separate held-out correct/incorrect, paired 30/57) is an empirical failure of this implicit-PRM read-out on rescued cliff trajectories; our adapter is trained on ~2 bridge pairs per question, not thousands of outcome-labelled responses, which is far below the data regime they use. They did NOT test single-question transient adapters, nor whether the token-level increments localize anything (only sequence-level reranking).
- **Process Reinforcement through Implicit Rewards (PRIME)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01456 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Online RL where the dense token reward is r_phi(y_t) = beta*log[pi_phi(y_t|y_<t)/pi_ref(y_t|y_<t)], with the implicit PRM pi_phi initialized from the SFT policy and continuously updated by CE on binary outcome labels of the policy's own rollouts. Advantage A_t = sum_{s>=t}[r_phi(y_s) - LOO mean over the K-1 other rollouts] + [r_o - LOO mean], gamma=1, process and outcome returns kept separate. Reference = SFT model, which beats a separately trained PRM. Cost: one extra model forward per rollout plus a PRM update step. Reports 2.5x sample efficiency and +6.9% over outcome-only RLOO, but no analysis of which tokens receive large process rewards.
  - relevance: Shows that online log-ratio credit is useful as an RL advantage shaper even if diffuse; it is a sequence-return shaper, not a token detector. Not our regime: they have abundant on-policy outcome-labelled rollouts per step; our cliff data is 120 examples/iteration at 1.6% of the mix and we train with SFT re-initialized from base. They did NOT examine rescued (off-policy, adapter-sampled) trajectories or a privileged teacher.
- **From r to Q*: Your Language Model is Secretly a Q-Function** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12358 (2024 (COLM)) ✓
  - mechanism: Derives DPO in the token-level MDP as inverse soft Q-learning: the per-token implicit reward beta*log[pi/pi_ref] satisfies a Bellman equation, so a DPO-trained model's token log-ratios are (up to a potential) per-token advantages. Claims DPO 'performs some type of credit assignment' (qualitative example of a trajectory where the log-ratio drops at an erroneous span) and that MCTS-style search is equivalent to likelihood search on the DPO policy (beam search improves the policy). Signal: pairwise preferences; cost: none beyond DPO.
  - relevance: Theoretical basis for M5 (reading a stage-2 DPO adapter's log-ratio as a PRM). Our measurement shows the adapter separates its own training pairs by 5.5 nats but the per-token signal does not discriminate held-out success/failure — i.e. the Q-function interpretation holds only where the DPO data covers the state space. They did NOT test generalization of the implicit credit to held-out samples of the same question, nor small-data adapters.
- **VinePPO: Refining Credit Assignment in RL Training of LLMs** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01679 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Replaces PPO's value network with unbiased MC value estimates: split the CoT into steps (newlines/punctuation, sub-split long steps at '='), and at each step boundary sample K=9 independent completions from the current policy; V_MC(s_t) = mean outcome. Advantages are MC-value differences between consecutive steps. Cost 2-5x per iteration but 3x less wall-clock to PPO's peak because updates are far better. Diagnostic: PPO's value net ranks five alternative next steps at ~chance (50%), MC ranks them correctly; value error grows along the chain for PPO and shrinks for MC.
  - relevance: The canonical evidence that learned/likelihood-based value signals are near-random at step ranking while MC is informative; this is the 'outcome-MC per-token credit' the researcher rejected as too heavy (rollouts from every prefix). Useful as the ceiling any cheap credit signal should be compared to. They did NOT study SFT-time weighting or an off-policy teacher.
- **Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08935 (2023) ✓
  - mechanism: Automatic step labels by MC completion: from each intermediate step s_i, a completer (LLemma-7B/MetaMATH) samples N=8 continuations; hard label = 1 if any reaches the gold answer, soft label = fraction. Train a PRM on ~170k GSM8K / ~270k MATH solutions so labelled, then use it for reranking and for step-by-step PPO (reward at each step end). Cost: ~N x #steps extra generations per training solution (implicit-PRM paper estimates ~80 trajectories per instruction).
  - relevance: Defines the expensive 'rollouts from every prefix' credit that was rejected for our loss. Qwen's later 'Lessons' paper shows these MC labels are noisy because they depend on the completer. Not applicable as a loss, but defines what a cheap proxy must approximate. They did NOT use gold solutions as a privileged teacher.
- **Dense Reward for Free in RLHF (Attention-Based Credit, ABC)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00782 (2024 (ICML)) ✓
  - mechanism: Redistribute the reward model's scalar r_C over tokens using its own last-layer attention (head-averaged) from the reward-readout position: R_hat(s,a,s') = beta*alpha_{I(s')}*r_C + (1-beta)*R(s,a,s'), with alpha_i the attention on token i. Proposition 3.1 shows this is potential-based shaping (Phi(s)=r_C*sum_{t<=I(s)} alpha_t) so optimal policies are unchanged. Cost: zero extra forward passes. Evidence of localization is illustrative (figure of attention highlighting); no evaluation that attended tokens are reasoning-critical.
  - relevance: Attention-based credit is 'free' but requires a reward model; our self-contained constraint leaves only the verifier (binary) and the base/adapter models, whose attention is not trained to predict correctness. Potential-based shaping argument also implies ABC changes optimization speed, not the optimum, which is not the issue for our SFT objective. They did NOT test reasoning tasks or show attention selects math tokens over style tokens.
- **Entropy-Regularized Process Reward Model (ER-PRM)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11006 (2024 (TMLR 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Replaces Math-Shepherd's mean-of-outcomes step label by the KL-regularized MDP soft value: r(a_[l],x) = (1/eta) log E_{completions ~ pi_0}[exp(eta * r(a,x))] from 16 sampled completions per step, i.e. a log-sum-exp (optimistic for eta>0) aggregation consistent with a KL-regularized policy. Trains an explicit PRM on these labels; best eta is 1-2 on hard data, 15 on easy. Cost same as Math-Shepherd (MC completions per step). Gains 1-3% in best-of-N over Math-Shepherd-style labels.
  - relevance: The 'entropy-regularized process reward' the task names; it is still MC-per-prefix and thus outside our budget. Its only conceptual contribution for us is that step value should be the soft (log-mean-exp) aggregate, not the mean. They did NOT use model entropy or likelihood contrast as the signal.
- **Right Question is Already Half the Answer: Fully Unsupervised LLM Reasoning Incentivization (EMPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05812 (2025 (NeurIPS)) (from memory)
  - mechanism: Sequence-level unsupervised RL: sample multiple answers per unlabeled question, cluster them semantically, and use cluster-frequency (predictive entropy in answer space) as the reward, minimizing entropy via GRPO. No token-level credit; the signal is self-consistency of final answers. Cost: a group of rollouts per prompt, same as GRPO.
  - relevance: Not token credit; listed because 'entropy-based credit' is sometimes taken to mean this family. Our verifier makes answer-consistency redundant. They did NOT do per-token weighting.
- **Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective RL for LLM Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01939 (2025 (NeurIPS)) ✓
  - mechanism: Measures per-token policy entropy in CoT: ~50% of tokens have entropy <1e-2 and only 20% exceed 0.672; high-entropy tokens are 'forks' (wait/however/unless/thus/since/suppose/assume/define), low-entropy ones are word suffixes, code fragments and expression components. RLVR barely changes entropy structure (>86% overlap of top-20% positions with base throughout training) and mainly raises entropy at forks. Method: mask the policy-gradient to the top rho=20% entropy tokens per batch; matches full-gradient on Qwen3-8B and beats it on 14B/32B (+11 AIME25 on 32B), while training on the 80% low-entropy tokens degrades.
  - relevance: Strongest published evidence that an intrinsic per-token signal (base entropy) localizes decision points and that learning on the remaining 80% is useless or harmful in RL. Our M1/M7 show adapter contrast g and entropy overlap only Jaccard 0.33, and M4 shows teacher disagreement on successes sits in the FIRST third; an entropy-masked SFT on rescued samples is the obvious published-under-another-name baseline. They did NOT test this in SFT/off-policy regimes or on cliff data, and their forks are connective/style-like words, which is exactly what our g+ mass lands on.
- **Segment Policy Optimization (SPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23564 (2025 (NeurIPS)) ✓
  - mechanism: Intermediate-granularity credit: partition the response into segments (SPO-chain: cutpoints at low-probability tokens; SPO-tree: tree-structured sampling for long CoT), estimate each segment's value by MC completions (chain-based or shared-tree), and apply the segment advantage to its tokens with a probability mask that confines the update to the segment's low-probability tokens. No critic. Cost: MC estimates per segment, reduced by tree sharing. Gains 6-12 pts on GSM8K vs PPO/GRPO and 7-11 on MATH500 (2K/4K context).
  - relevance: Shows segment-level MC credit is the practical compromise, and that restricting updates to low-probability tokens within a segment works. Still needs MC completions (rejected here). They did NOT address off-policy rescued samples or SFT.
- **Rewarding Progress: Scaling Automated Process Verifiers for LLM Reasoning (PAVs)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08146 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Defines the process reward as progress = advantage of a step under a separate 'prover' policy mu: A_mu(s,a) = Q_mu(s,a) - V_mu(s), estimated by MC under the prover. Theory characterizes good provers as complementary/different from the base policy (even weaker ones); a plain Q/V under the same policy gives little. Beam search with PAVs gives 8-10% and 1.5-5x compute gains over ORMs; PAV-RL 5-6x sample efficiency. Cost: prover rollouts per step.
  - relevance: Conceptually closest to using a privileged/adapter policy as a 'prover': credit should be progress under a complementary policy, not likelihood under the learner. Our adapter and privileged-base are candidate provers, but we only have their likelihoods, not their per-prefix success rates; M2 shows adapter likelihood is not success-discriminative. They did NOT use likelihood contrast as progress.
- **Stop Summation: Min-Form Credit Assignment Is All Process Reward Model Needs for Reasoning (PURE)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15275 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: With a PRM in RL, the standard value = sum of gamma-discounted future step rewards lets the policy hack by inserting many high-reward steps; PURE defines the value as the MINIMUM over future step rewards, which bounds the value and makes advantage concentrate on the worst step. PRM is a standard step-scorer; reports comparable accuracy to verifiable-reward RL using only 30% of steps and 82.5% AMC23 with 10% verifiable rewards; summation-form collapses training from the start.
  - relevance: Supports our M4 reading that the informative statistic is the worst window (min/CVaR-tail), not the mean; our C(y) already uses a CVaR tail of student NLL. They did NOT use likelihood-contrast or a privileged teacher as the PRM.
- **KTAE: A Model-Free Algorithm to Key-Tokens Advantage Estimation in Mathematical Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16826 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: For each token type in a GRPO group, build a 2x2 table of (appears / does not appear) x (correct / incorrect rollouts); compute a Fisher exact-test association and an information gain, with a sign D for direction; token advantage = A_GRPO + sigmoid(h1*F + h2*IG)*D - 0.5. No extra models; cost is counting. Qualitative: content words like 'complement', 'ratio' flagged positive, generic 'First', 'denote' negative; trains shorter, more accurate responses.
  - relevance: A cheap, self-contained success-vs-failure contrast at the token-type level, using exactly the ~2-3 correct / ~20 incorrect samples per question we have; our M2 did the analogous contrast with adapter-g and found no discrimination, but KTAE contrasts token presence, not likelihood. They did NOT apply it to SFT weighting or to off-policy samples, and their signal is vocabulary-level not position-level.
- **Critical Tokens Matter: Token-Level Contrastive Estimation Enhances LLM's Reasoning Capability (cDPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19943 (2024 (ICML 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Fine-tune a positive model on one correct trajectory per problem and a negative model on frequent incorrect ones; per-token score log s_t = (1+beta) log P_pos(y_t) - beta log P_neg(y_t) - log Z (beta=1), low score = critical. In DPO, weight the rejected trajectory's tokens by (1-s_t) so only critical tokens of the negative are pushed down. Validation: replacing a single critical token (e.g. 'owed') and rolling out raises pass@1 to ~30% and pass@64 to >90% on 100 failed trajectories.
  - relevance: Likelihood contrast between two fine-tuned models as token credit, applied only on the NEGATIVE side; consistent with our M4 finding that contrast is a wrong-turn detector on failures and weak on successes. Suggests the cliff-specific objective should be asymmetric (penalize localized failure tokens, uniform on successes). They did NOT report what the contrast does on the chosen side, nor use a privileged same-model teacher.
- **Rho-1: Not All Tokens Are What You Need (Selective Language Modeling)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07965 (2024 (NeurIPS)) ✓
  - mechanism: Score every token by excess loss L_theta(x_i) - L_ref(x_i) where the reference is the same architecture trained on a small curated corpus; train only on the top k% (60-70%). Token loss dynamics: 51% L->L, 26% H->L (useful), 11% H->H, 12% L->H (noise). Selected tokens are predominantly math-content tokens; custom symbols, gibberish and boilerplate are dropped. Up to +30% few-shot on 9 math tasks with 3% of tokens.
  - relevance: The SFT-time analogue of likelihood-contrast weighting (student-minus-reference loss), and a published example where contrast DID select content tokens — but the reference is a high-quality-corpus model, not a single-question adapter, so the contrast is distributional, not stylistic. Our class-B tokens (high surprisal, adapter did not help) are their H->H. They did NOT use an adapter or gold-conditioned reference, and the selection is for pretraining data, not reasoning trajectories.
- **The Unlocking Spell on Base LLMs: Rethinking Alignment via In-Context Learning (URIAL)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01552 (2023 (ICLR 2024)) ✓
  - mechanism: Token-distribution-shift analysis between base and aligned models: decode with the aligned model, score each position under both; base and aligned agree on the majority of positions, and the shifted positions are predominantly stylistic tokens (discourse markers, transitional phrases, safety disclaimers such as 'Hello', 'However', 'Remember'). Conclusion: alignment mostly learns style; knowledge comes from the base, so 3 in-context examples suffice.
  - relevance: The original 'likelihood shift concentrates on style tokens' result; our M1 (top-10% g tokens are 'We', 'Step', '---', ' $') replicates it for a transient adapter. Implies adapter-vs-base contrast will always be dominated by format unless projected away from style. They did NOT analyse reasoning correctness or RL-trained models.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning for LLM Multi-Step Reasoning (OREO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16145 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Jointly train a policy and an explicit value V_phi from unpaired trajectories with sparse terminal reward by enforcing the soft Bellman equation V*(s_t) = R - beta*sum_{i>=t} log[pi(a_i|s_i)/pi_ref(a_i|s_i)] at every token/step (DPO is the t=0 special case); MSE value loss plus a policy loss on the Bellman residual with stop-gradients. Step-level and response-level variants. Case studies show the explicit value drops at the erroneous state (negative advantage) more sharply than the implicit policy log-ratio does; value also guides tree search.
  - relevance: Directly reports that the implicit (policy log-ratio) estimate is weaker at localizing the error than an explicitly learned value, aligning with our M5. Uses unpaired successes AND failures, which we have (2-3 correct, ~20 incorrect per converted cliff). They did NOT work at our data scale (a value head trained on ~100 questions is not viable) or with a privileged teacher.
- **On the Generalization of SFT: A RL Perspective with Reward Rectification (Dynamic Fine-Tuning, DFT)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05629 (2025 (ICLR 2026)) ✓
  - mechanism: Shows SFT's gradient equals a policy gradient with implicit reward 1/pi(y_t), which over-weights low-probability tokens; DFT multiplies each token's CE loss by stop_grad(pi_theta(y_t|y_<t)), i.e. down-weights surprising tokens. One-line change; consistent gains over SFT on math/code/multimodal and competitive in offline RL settings.
  - relevance: A token-weighting SFT rule using only the student's own probability — exactly the signal behind our C(y) learnability score, but applied per token. Our class-B tokens (76-78% of loss mass, surprisal >1 nat) are what DFT suppresses; on rescued samples DFT would effectively train only on the already-likely 'scaffold'. They did NOT study rescued/off-policy samples, and ASFT shows DFT drifts without a KL anchor.
- **Anchored Supervised Fine-Tuning (ASFT)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23753 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Interprets DFT as reward-weighted regression lacking distributional anchoring; ASFT keeps DFT's token-probability reweighting and adds a lightweight KL regularizer to the base model. Outperforms SFT and DFT on math, medical and code with minimal overhead.
  - relevance: Relevant to our STaR-style re-init from base: a base-anchored, probability-weighted SFT on rescued samples is a published objective. They did NOT treat cliff/off-policy data specially.
- **VCORE: Variance-Controlled Optimization-based Reweighting for Chain-of-Thought Supervision** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27462 (2025 (ACL 2026)) ✓
  - mechanism: Reformulates CoT SFT as a constrained optimization that adaptively allocates per-token supervision weights under a variance constraint, driven by each token's contribution to the trajectory loss; strongest gains on lower-capacity models (Qwen3-4B) and as an RL initialization. Exact weight rule not in abstract.
  - relevance: Another student-only token reweighting for CoT SFT on our model family; a natural baseline for any cliff-specific weighting. They did NOT use a teacher/adapter contrast.
- **Supervised Fine Tuning on Curated Data is Reinforcement Learning (and can be improved) (iw-SFT)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12856 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Shows SFT on verifier-filtered data maximizes a lower bound on the sparse-reward RL objective; iw-SFT tightens the bound with sequence-level importance weights (ratio of current policy to the reference/behaviour policy, clipped), and extends to quality-scored data. Reaches 66.7% AIME24.
  - relevance: Formalizes our build_dataset->SFT loop and says the missing correction is SEQUENCE-level, matching our M6 finding that off-policy-ness of rescued samples is a sequence property (token-level IS is inert). A per-trajectory weight (e.g., from C(y) or adapter/base sequence log-ratio) is published here under the iw-SFT name. They did NOT study cliff-only data or transient adapters.
- **Do Not Let Low-Probability Tokens Over-Dominate in RL for LLMs (Advantage Reweighting / Lopti)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12929 (2025 (ICLR 2026)) ✓
  - mechanism: Proves the per-token gradient norm scales with (1-pi(y_t)), so low-probability tokens dominate updates; fixes: Advantage Reweighting A_t <- [alpha*pi(y_t) + (1-alpha)]*A_t, and Lopti, a two-stage update (low-prob tokens first, then high-prob). Up to +46% on K&K logic puzzles with GRPO.
  - relevance: RL-side twin of DFT; quantifies why uniform loss on rescued samples is dominated by class-B high-surprisal tokens. They did NOT test SFT or off-policy data.
- **Sparse but Critical: A Token-Level Analysis of Distributional Shifts in RLVR Fine-Tuning of LLMs** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22446 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Per-position divergence between base and RLVR policies on the same contexts: >83% (DAPO) to >98% (SimpleRL) of positions show near-zero divergence; shifted tokens are function words, reasoning terms and some equation fragments (numerals/operators unchanged), concentrated near the beginning and modestly at the end of responses, and not purely high-entropy. Cross-sampling: injecting <4% RL-chosen tokens into base decoding recovers RL accuracy; replacing ~5% high-divergence positions in RL decoding collapses it.
  - relevance: 2026 evidence that the tokens that matter are sparse, early-positioned, and lexically 'plausible/style-like' yet causally decisive — so our M1 observation that g+ mass sits on openers/connectives may not be 'just style'. Their causal test (token swap + re-decode) is the falsification our M3 lacked. They did NOT analyse SFT, adapters, or cliff questions.
- **Why is Your Language Model a Poor Implicit Reward Model?** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07981 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Theory plus experiments showing implicit reward models (log-ratio of a DPO-trained LM) rely on superficial token-level cues and generalize worse than explicit reward heads under token-level distribution shift, refuting the 'generation is harder than verification' explanation.
  - relevance: Published statement that log-ratio credit is surface-token-driven — consistent with M1/M5 that adapter log-ratios track style and do not separate held-out successes. They did NOT examine per-token localization in math CoT.
- **Unleashing Implicit Rewards: Prefix-Value Learning for Distribution-Level Optimization (IPVRM / DistRL)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13197 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Diagnoses a train-inference mismatch in implicit PRMs: training constrains only the sequence-level aggregate, so token-level credits are 'weakly identified' and can amplify misleading advantages. IPVRM instead learns P(eventual correctness | prefix) directly from outcome labels and derives step signals as TD differences between consecutive prefix values; DistRL uses these as advantages. Improves ProcessBench step verification.
  - relevance: Direct 2026 confirmation that implicit log-ratio token credit is diffuse/uninformative — the same conclusion as our M5 — and that prefix-value (not token log-ratio) is the identifiable object. They did NOT address tiny-data adapters or privileged teachers.
- **Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Step Rewards (iStar)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19199 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Uses an implicit PRM trained by a trajectory-level DPO objective but reads rewards at STEP granularity: r(o_1:t,a_t) = beta*log[pi_phi(a_t|.)/pi_old(a_t|.)], because token-level implicit rewards 'amplify variance and destabilize training as trajectories grow'.
  - relevance: Another report that token-level implicit credit is too noisy for long trajectories (ours are ~5k tokens); coarsening to step/segment is the published fix. Agentic domain, not math.
- **GRPO is Secretly a Process Reward Model (lambda-GRPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21154 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Shows that whenever rollouts in a GRPO group share prefixes, the group-relative objective is equivalent to PRM-aware RL with an on-policy MC process reward on the shared sub-trajectories; identifies an imbalance flaw when steps/rewards are uneven and proposes lambda-GRPO, which trains faster and better at negligible cost.
  - relevance: Explains why 'ORM as PRM' holds for free in group sampling; our rescued samples (24 per question from one adapter) share openings, so a group-relative SFT weighting across them would implicitly be MC step credit. They did NOT handle off-policy samples.
- **Exploiting Tree Structure for Credit Assignment in RL Training of LLMs (TEMPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18314 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Builds a prefix tree from the group's sampled responses; V(s_t) = mean outcome over descendants sharing the prefix; advantage = group-normalized MC signal plus a branch-gated TD correction that is zero at non-branching tokens (reduces to GRPO) and non-zero at divergence points. No value net or PRM; 1.4-1.6x faster than GRPO to comparable accuracy; 91.0 vs 87.6 MATH on Qwen3-4B.
  - relevance: Free step-credit from shared prefixes among samples of the same question; applicable to our 24 adapter samples per cliff without extra rollouts, yielding credit only at branch points. They did NOT apply it off-policy/in SFT.
- **GRPO-lambda: Credit Assignment improves LLM Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00194 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Adds eligibility traces to GRPO: a critic-free TD-error approximation built from token-level log-probabilities after generation, with several lambda-return weightings; +3-4.5 points average on AIME24/MATH500/Olympiad/Minerva/AMC for 1.5-7B models.
  - relevance: Token-level credit from the policy's own log-probs only; relevant as a self-contained shaper but unvalidated for localization. They did NOT analyse which tokens receive credit.
- **GTPO and GRPO-S: Token and Sequence-Level Reward Shaping with Policy Entropy** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04349 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Dynamic entropy weighting: GTPO assigns each token a reward proportional to its normalized entropy H_{i,t}/sum_k H_{k,t} within the group and detects gradient conflicts; GRPO-S applies the entropy weight at sequence level for long-CoT stability.
  - relevance: Entropy-as-credit again; our M7 shows base entropy and surprisal largely coincide (Jaccard 0.75) on rescued samples, so entropy-weighting would up-weight class-B tokens. They did NOT evaluate off-policy SFT.
- **Rethinking Token-Level Credit Assignment in RLVR: A Polarity-Entropy Analysis (EAPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11056 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Proves token credit is upper-bounded by token entropy and decomposes tokens into four quadrants (positive/negative x high/low entropy): reinforcing high-entropy tokens on successes generalizes; penalizing high-entropy tokens on failures prunes errors; positive low-entropy reinforcement gives transient gains then entropy collapse. EAPO: A_t <- A_t + alpha*sign(A_t)*clip((H_t-mu)/sigma, ±|A_t|/phi), alpha=0.2.
  - relevance: Gives a principled reason that 'class B' (low-entropy for the adapter? no: high-surprisal) vs forks matters by polarity: for our SUCCESS-only SFT, the implication is to up-weight high-entropy tokens and not sharpen low-entropy ones. They did NOT test SFT or a privileged teacher.
- **ACPO: Asymmetric Credit Policy Optimization via Mode-Local Entropy Surrogate** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03126 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Replaces global entropy (corrupted by vocabulary tail) with 1 - p(top token) as a mode-local uncertainty proxy, and applies it asymmetrically: emphasize uncertain decisions on successful trajectories, penalize confident predictions on failed ones, with mismatch routing and saturation correction; beats 80/20 masking, GTPO, DAPO, SAPO on AIME25/HumanEval Pro.
  - relevance: Published asymmetric success/failure credit rule using only the policy's own distribution — a self-contained candidate for weighting our 2-3 correct and ~20 incorrect samples per cliff. Not tested in SFT or off-policy.
- **DGPO: Distribution-Guided Policy Optimization for Fine-Grained Credit Assignment** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03327 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Reinterprets the per-token deviation from the reference (bounded Hellinger distance instead of KL) gated by policy entropy as an exploration score, and reallocates the sequence advantage to tokens in proportion to the gated score; 3.6% overhead; Qwen2.5-32B 60.0 Avg@32 AIME24 vs DAPO 50.0.
  - relevance: Token credit = (policy-vs-reference divergence) x entropy — the same ingredients as our g_t and entropy, used to reallocate a sequence reward rather than as a detector. Their gains suggest this works as a shaper even if not a localizer. Not SFT/off-policy.
- **When Implausible Tokens Get Reinforced: Tail-Aware Credit Calibration (TACO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07976 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Names 'positive-credit contamination': correct rollouts contain locally erroneous/implausible low-probability tokens that receive the same positive advantage. Tail-risk score r_tail = -log p_t - H_t + log alpha (surprisal in excess of local entropy); weight w_t = 1 - lambda(1-exp(-r_tail)) for r_tail>0 else 1. Only ~1.9% of tokens are down-weighted yet gains are consistent across 3 LLMs/8 benchmarks; synthetic MDP shows oracle step credit beats broadcast more as traces lengthen.
  - relevance: Directly targets our class-B problem on SUCCESSES (76-78% of SFT loss mass on high-surprisal tokens): a surprisal-minus-entropy excess score is a self-contained detector of 'lucky' tokens in rescued successes. Their score uses only the student model. Not tested in SFT; not with adapters.
- **Outcome-Grounded Advantage Reshaping for Fine-Grained Credit Assignment in Mathematical Reasoning (OAR)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07408 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Redistributes the GRPO sequence advantage by each token's contribution to the final-answer distribution: OAR-P masks the token ([PAD]) and measures KL shift of the answer distribution (4.2x overhead), OAR-G uses gradient x input of a self-distillation loss under embedding noise (1.4x). AIME25 Pass@1 9.4 -> 12.2 (Qwen2.5-7B).
  - relevance: Counterfactual attribution from a single model without rollouts — a middle ground between per-prefix MC (rejected) and likelihood contrast (diffuse). Could be computed on our rescued samples with the base model. They did NOT test SFT or report token-type breakdowns.
- **Guidance Contrastive Token Credit Assignment for Discrete Policy Optimization (GCPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29198 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Per-token credit eta_{i,t} = KL(pi(y_t|x, y_<t) || pi(y_t|x^-, y_<t)) between the model's predictions under a positive prompt and a negative prompt (e.g., 'generate a wrong answer'); histogram-equalize and multiply the sample advantage: A_{i,t} = eta_norm * A_i. Two forward passes per token. Credit concentrates on prompt-relevant objects in T2I and on discriminative reasoning tokens in multimodal QA.
  - relevance: Same-model prompt-contrast credit, i.e. the mechanism behind our privileged contrast g' (gold-in-prompt vs bare). They use it only to RESCALE a sequence advantage (magnitude, not sign), which sidesteps our finding that g' is mostly negative; note their evidence of localization is in vision tasks, not math CoT.
- **OPPO: Bayesian Value Recursion for Token-Level Credit Assignment in LLM Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21851 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Treats the per-token oracle/privileged discrimination signal (log ratio of an oracle-prompted model vs the student) as a Bayesian likelihood-ratio update of the belief that the trajectory will succeed; accumulating it gives a closed-form running success-probability at every position with one extra forward pass, no value net or rollouts. First-order analysis: advantage = per-token discrimination x a state weight that concentrates on pivotal tokens. Self-oracle (same model) and teacher-oracle variants; +6.0 AMC23, +5.2 AIME24, gains grow with length.
  - relevance: The most direct published use of our privileged contrast g' as token credit, but used cumulatively as a prefix-value (sequence-level belief) rather than as a local weight — consistent with our M4 that negative mass discriminates at the sequence level. They did NOT validate on cliff/rescued off-policy samples or in SFT.
- **Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLMs (OPSD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: One model is both teacher and student: teacher conditions on the question plus a verified solution s*, student on the question only; minimize per-token (clipped forward) KL between p(.|x,y_<t) and p(.|x,s*,y_<t) over the student's own rollouts. Dense full-vocabulary supervision with no reward model; better token efficiency than RL and better than off-policy distillation on math.
  - relevance: This is our 'privileged' scoring mode turned into a loss: per-token KL to the base model shown y*. Our M4 shows that signal is mostly negative (teacher disagrees) on sampled trajectories and discriminates only at sequence level; the 2026 follow-ups below document its failure modes. They did NOT restrict to cliffs, use adapter-rescued samples, or analyse where the KL mass lands.
- **Anti-Self-Distillation for Reasoning RL via Pointwise Mutual Information (AntiSD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11609 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Derives the OPSD per-token signal as u_t = log pi(y_t|x,c,y_<t) - log pi(y_t|x,y_<t) = PMI(y_t; privileged context c | x,y_<t). Empirically the privileged context inflates confidence on tokens already implied by the solution (connectives, verifiable claims) and deflates deliberation tokens ('Wait','Let','Maybe'), so minimizing the divergence concentrates signal on structural tokens. AntiSD ascends a JS divergence instead (bounded at 1/2 log 2 on the over-sampled side), with an entropy gate when teacher entropy collapses; reaches GRPO accuracy in 2-10x fewer steps and +2.1 to +11.5 pts over GRPO across 4B-30B models, while default self-distillation underperforms GRPO on every model.
  - relevance: A published, token-level diagnosis of exactly our g'_t: it is PMI with the gold solution and its positive mass is structural/style — matching our M4 token-kind findings. Implication: do not use g'+ as a weight on successes; its sign structure is about solution-implied tokens, not reasoning quality. They did NOT consider rescued off-policy samples or SFT.
- **Rethinking On-Policy Self-Distillation for Thinking Models** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05184 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Shows privileged OPSD degrades thinking models on long traces (up to -17% on math competitions): the gold-conditioned teacher lowers fork rates and specifically penalizes sampled reconsideration/verification/backtracking tokens that an un-privileged teacher supports, so the student loses self-correction behaviour; privileged context helps instruction-tuned models but hurts stronger thinkers.
  - relevance: Explains why a privileged-teacher token weight is dangerous on 5k-token rescued traces: it punishes exploration tokens. Together with AntiSD, this closes off 'weight successes by teacher agreement'. Not cliff-specific.
- **When Are Teacher Tokens Reliable? Position-Weighted On-Policy Self-Distillation (PW-OPSD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21606 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Measures which privileged-teacher tokens are reliable: local uncertainty/entropy scores predict reliability at AUROC <= 0.57 (cannot separate genuine uncertainty from benign solution diversity), while an oriented within-sequence position score reaches AUROC 0.83 — reliability increases along the trajectory. PW-OPSD multiplies the per-token KL by an increasing position weight; +1.0/+1.1 AIME24/25 Avg@12 with no extra teacher compute.
  - relevance: Independent confirmation of our M4 LOCATION finding (successes' disagreement sits in the first third where the approach legitimately differs from gold; teacher is trustworthy late). A position-ramped privileged weight is therefore already published. They did NOT examine rescued samples or failure localization.
- **Localizing Credit at the Divergence: Path-Conditioned Self-Distillation for LLM Reasoning (HSD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15576 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Answer-conditioned self-distillation yields weak intermediate signal on terse-answer tasks; Hindsight Self-Distillation conditions the teacher on a successful PEER rollout from the same group rather than the gold answer, so the per-token KL concentrates at the divergence position tau where the failed rollout first departs from the successful one. +4.1/+3.7 AIME24/25 over OPSD on Qwen3-8B; +7.3 AIME24 over GRPO on Qwen3-32B.
  - relevance: Uses our exact available signals (a verifier-correct sibling sample as the privileged context) and credits failures at the divergence point; for cliffs the 'successful peer' is the rescued sample itself. This is close to a published version of 'learn rescued trajectories by contrasting against own failures' and must be cited. They did NOT use a transient adapter, nor SFT.
- **DemoPSD: Disagreement-Modulated Policy Self-Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02502 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Per-token blending between student and privileged teacher controlled by their disagreement: the target is a reverse-KL barycenter whose mixing weight at each position depends on teacher-student divergence, so the student adopts teacher guidance selectively, mitigating privileged-information leakage and entropy collapse. Beats GRPO and SDPO on SciKnowEval with higher training entropy and OOD gains on GPQA.
  - relevance: Published 'weight the privileged signal by disagreement magnitude' — the natural next step after our M4; their motivation (leakage of answer-dependent shortcuts) is the same leakage our bridge gates target. Not math-cliff-specific; not SFT.
- **Self-Distilled Policy Gradient (SDPG)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04036 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: L = L_outcome + beta(k)*L_OPD+ + alpha*KL(pi, pi_ref); the full-vocabulary reverse-KL distillation term sum_a p(a|x,y_<t) log[p/q] to the privileged teacher q(.|c,x,y_<t) is gated to rollouts with positive outcome advantage (m_i = 1[A_i>0]). Proposition: the token advantage under distillation is SG[D_bar_t - log(p_t(a)/q_t(a))]. Keeps entropy >0.4 where RLSD collapses; AIME24 0.408 on Qwen3-4B.
  - relevance: On our model size; shows privileged distillation should be gated to verifier-correct trajectories — which is what restricting to rescued successes does — and combined with an outcome term. They did NOT restrict to cliffs or use adapter samples.
- **Self-Distilled RLVR (RLSD)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03128 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Reports that learning purely from the privileged teacher causes severe information leakage and instability; RLSD uses the token-level student-teacher difference to set update MAGNITUDE while verifier outcome sets the DIRECTION.
  - relevance: Same design principle as GCPO/DGPO: use contrast for magnitude only, verifier for sign — a reading compatible with our M4/M6 (contrast is not sign-informative on successes). Work-in-progress paper.
- **HDPO: Hybrid Distillation Policy Optimization via Privileged Self-Distillation** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23871 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Defines cliffs as prompts where all G=16 rollouts fail; for them, sample from pi_theta(.|x ⊕ y*) (same model with the gold solution in context), keep verifier-correct trajectories, and train the student pi_theta(.|x) on those OFF-policy privileged trajectories with a per-token Jensen-Shannon loss to the teacher distribution pi_theta(.|x ⊕ y*) (top-64 logits + tail), uniformly over tokens; total loss L_GRPO + lambda*L_JSD, lambda in {0.01,0.1}. Proposition 2: rejection-sampling with R=1 recovers the beta->0 KL-regularized optimal policy. Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: pass@8 +1.4-1.7, pass@4 up to +1.1, pass@1 -2.3 at lambda=0.1; no token-level analysis.
  - relevance: The closest published sibling of our setting: same cliff definition, same bridge trajectories (base + gold in prompt, verifier-filtered), and a student objective on them. Differences: they distill the teacher's full per-token distribution on the bridge trajectories themselves (our STAGED instead fits a transient adapter on bridges and learns the adapter's gold-free samples), and they report no token-level credit analysis. Any 'learn bridge samples via privileged-teacher KL' idea is already published here; novelty must be in weighting/selection of rescued (gold-free) samples.
- **Self-Explore: Enhancing Mathematical Reasoning with Fine-grained Rewards** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10346 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Find the 'first pit' in a failed solution by sampling k completions from each step and locating the first step from which no completion succeeds; build pairs with rejected = the pit step (and its prefix) and chosen = a correct continuation, then DPO. +11.6 GSM8K / +2.9 MATH over SFT.
  - relevance: Step-level credit on failures via MC (cost = rollouts per step), and prefix-conditioned pairs — both outside our constraints (anchors abandoned, MC rejected). Cited only as the origin of the 'first error step' idea that cheaper methods (Unsupervised PRMs, HSD) now approximate.
- **Step-Controlled DPO (SCDPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00782 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Construct negatives that are correct up to step k and then diverge by re-sampling from step k at higher temperature; standard DPO loss; qualitative analysis shows the implicit reward drops at the injected error. 88.5 GSM8K / 58.1 MATH on InternLM2-20B.
  - relevance: Segment-credit DPO without a PRM; prefix-sharing pairs are exactly the anchor methodology we abandoned, so not proposable. They did NOT use privileged teachers.
- **Step-level Value Preference Optimization (SVPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10858 (2024 (EMNLP Findings)) ✓
  - mechanism: MCTS annotates step-level preferences; an explicit value model is trained learning-to-rank style to mimic the implicit reward, combined with a step-level DPO loss; SOTA on in/out-of-domain math.
  - relevance: MC-based step DPO; too heavy and prefix-based. Not applicable.
- **Step-DPO: Step-wise Preference Optimization for Long-chain Reasoning** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18629 (2024) (from memory)
  - mechanism: Treat individual reasoning steps as preference units: given a shared prefix, chosen = correct next step, rejected = the erroneous step (located with GPT-4 / verification); 10K pairs suffice for large gains.
  - relevance: Uses an external judge for error localization and prefix-conditioned pairs — violates both hard constraints.
- **Token-level Direct Preference Optimization (TDPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11999 (2024 (ICML)) (from memory)
  - mechanism: Rewrites DPO with a per-token forward-KL constraint (sequential KL divergence term) under a token-level Bradley-Terry model, controlling per-token divergence from the reference rather than sentence-level only.
  - relevance: Token-level regularization, not credit; it does not decide which tokens matter.
- **TGDPO: Harnessing Token-Level Reward Guidance for Enhancing DPO** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14574 (2025 (ICML)) ✓
  - mechanism: Decomposes sequence-level PPO into token-level proximal problems, derives a closed-form token-level optimal policy given an external token reward, and plugs it into a Bradley-Terry loss so tokens deviate from the reference in proportion to their reward.
  - relevance: Needs an external token reward model; its follow-ups (Se-DPO) report DPO's own implicit token reward lands on punctuation/line breaks. Not self-contained.
- **Se-DPO: Self-Evolving Token Credit for Direct Preference Optimization** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09568 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Notes DPO sums token log-ratios uniformly and that the implicit reward assigns disproportionate weight to non-critical tokens (punctuation, line breaks); derives optimal per-token credit c_t proportional to |r_t*|/sigma_t^2 with reference entropy as the noise proxy, learned by a small network during training; ~7% overhead, +8 win-rate on AlpacaEval 2 vs TGDPO.

- **Token-weighted Direct Preference Optimization with Attention (TwDPO / AttentionPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21883 (2026) ✓

Top-weighted tokens are judgment words (Yes/True/No/False) and negations.
  - relevance: Self-contained attention credit (reference model judges), but the highlighted tokens are verdict/negation words, i.e. surface cues — more evidence that attention/likelihood-derived credit is stylistic. Not math.
- **Token-Importance Guided Direct Preference Optimization (TI-DPO)** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19653 (2025) (from memory)

  - relevance: Gradient-attribution weighting is self-contained but unvalidated for reasoning localization.
- **Unsupervised Process Reward Models** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10158 (2026) ✓

  - relevance: Self-contained, label-free first-error localization from probabilities across sibling trajectories — our 24 samples per cliff are such a batch. Candidate failure-side signal; unverified on off-policy adapter samples.

## Family: Token/span-localized negatives & preference losses (39 papers)

### Scout synthesis

What this family says, read against the measurements.

1. Localized-negative objectives come in four mechanically distinct kinds, and each has a published owner. (a) Shared-prefix, step-masked pairs where the rejected gradient is restricted to one step or the post-divergence suffix: Step-DPO (GPT-4/human localizer), SCDPO (error manufactured by temperature at step k, verifier-only), Self-Explore (k=4 MC from each step, model-only), MCTS-DPO/SVPO/OmegaPRM (tree search + rollouts), SRPO (self-prompted first-wrong-thought + suffix resampling, prefix masked), Marco-o1 v2 (zero the common-prefix tokens in the DPO mask). All of these are prefix-conditioned continuation methods, i.e. the abandoned anchor methodology, and the ones that are self-contained (Self-Explore, SRPO, MCTS) pay with per-prefix rollouts the researcher ruled out. (b) Token-weighted preference losses without a shared prefix, where a per-token score gates the rejected side: cDPO (pos-model vs neg-model contrastive log-ratio, rejected side only), TIS-DPO (contrastive LLMs incl. a prompt-contrast variant), SePO (bottom-k% tokens of the rejected by an oracle-DPO log-ratio), SparsePO (learned masks), TGDPO (external token RM), OREAL (outcome-trained token credit omega- on negatives), Full-Step-DPO (softmax of -PRM reward over rejected steps), Step-KTO (unpaired, 70B-judge step labels). (c) Unlocalized negative reinforcement: NSR/W-REINFORCE, NFT, UFT unlikelihood, TOPR, AsymRE, POPO — all explicitly choose NOT to localize and rely on the gradient's own pi_v(1-pi_v) shape to redistribute mass. (d) Protection/displacement-aware masks that REMOVE negative gradient from tokens shared with successes: NTHR (hidden-state similarity), ResRL (projection residual onto positive subspace), GTPO conflict tokens, DaGRPO/CHES pair filtering, DPOP hinge. Entropy-capacity methods (80/20, HAPO, GTPO, ICT, STAPO, ConfPO) localize both signs by the student's own statistics and disagree on whether negatives belong on high-entropy forks (HAPO) or confident errors (GTPO).

2. Which of the candidate ideas are already published under another name. 'Weight rejected tokens by a contrast between a model that has seen successes and one that has seen failures' = cDPO / TIS-DPO(S)/(D); the M3 'scaffold+outcome contrast' is an SePO/OREAL-style selection whose score happened to be uninformative. 'Weight tokens by the same model under a different prompt' = TIS-DPO(P) contrastive prompts, but no one has used the GOLD SOLUTION in the prompt as the contrast; the privileged contrast g'_t is a new instance of that estimator. 'Mask the shared prefix / penalize only the post-divergence suffix' = SCDPO, Marco-o1 v2, SRPO. 'Use only successes from the scaffold and drop its failures' = LSPO itself, and is independently prescribed by TOPR (sequence ratio of an adapter failure under the base is exp(-63..-200) ~ 0 after [0,1] truncation) and AsymRE (off-policy updates should weight positives). 'Negative-only / unpaired unlikelihood term on verifier failures inside the SFT loss' = UFT / NSR / NFT. 'Emphasize low-probability tokens of off-policy successes' = LUFFY policy shaping. 'Read a token-level PRM off a trained log-ratio' = PRIME / From-r-to-Q* / RTO, which M5 shows does not work from a 4-step adapter. 'Protect rejected tokens that look like success tokens' = NTHR/ResRL.

3. Implications for the objective choice. (i) The measurements align with the family's own negative results: adapter-vs-base contrast (M1, M2) is a diffuse style shift, a DPO-adapter implicit PRM is uninformative (M5), token-level IS is inert (M6). Nothing in the family contradicts these; cDPO's contrast is a different estimator (success-trained vs failure-trained models) and remains unmeasured in this setting. (ii) The only self-contained localizer with demonstrated signal on the researcher's data is the privileged teacher's NEGATIVE mass on FAILURES (M4: p=6e-4 and 3e-6, concentrated in the last third = the wrong turn). The family's prior for that signal's value is strong (Step-DPO/Self-Explore gain +2.8-3.6 over whole-sequence DPO from correct localization; SRPO 28.7 vs 16.3 pass when the reset is clean), and it needs no rollouts and no shared prefix (it is a one-pass teacher-forced score). So the natural contribution is an UNPAIRED objective on cliffs: uniform (LUFFY-shaped or plain) SFT on rescued successes plus a g'-localized unlikelihood/NSR term on failures, with the negative mass gated by the privileged disagreement span rather than applied uniformly. (iii) Off-policy arithmetic says which failures to penalize: TOPR/AsymRE/LSPO all argue adapter-sampled failures carry ~zero usable negative weight; the base policy's own 8/8 rollout failures (and, in later iterations, the student's own) are the on-policy negatives, and they are exactly what M4 analyzed. (iv) Likelihood displacement is the main hazard: successes and failures share token profiles (M2), so any negative gradient must either be span-localized by g' (the family's Step-DPO argument: do not punish the correct prefix), or protected NTHR/ResRL-style on tokens that the successes also use, or both; DPOP-style hinge on the successes' likelihood is a cheap guard. (v) Positive side: M4 shows the teacher's agreement does not discriminate, and successes' disagreement sits in the first third (a different approach from gold, not an error), so g' must NOT be used to weight or penalize successes — consistent with cDPO weighting only the rejected side. (vi) Detectability: Step-DPO used 10K pairs and <500 steps as a separate short stage, not a 1.6% mix term; the cliff objective should be run as its own small phase (or a LoRA stage) if it is to be measurable. (vii) Untested in the whole family: transfer of a localized-negative signal from cliff set A to held-out cliff set B, and any use of a gold-conditioned teacher-forced contrast as the localizer — those are the open, self-contained contributions.

### Papers

- **Step-DPO: Step-wise Preference Optimization for Long-chain Reasoning of LLMs** — arXiv:2406.18629 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Loss is standard DPO but the unit is a single reasoning step: -log sigma(beta log pi(s_win|x,s_1..k-1)/pi_ref - beta log pi(s_lose|x,s_1..k-1)/pi_ref), i.e. the log-ratio is computed only over the one step after a shared correct prefix. Error location: 'verify the correctness of each reasoning step until we find the first error and record its step number k. This process can be done manually or using GPT-4'. Chosen step is self-generated: resample from pi_ref given x and the correct prefix s_1..k-1, keep continuations whose final answer matches ground truth. Motivation quoted: 'the first error often appears midway... Rejecting an entire undesirable answer in DPO may also discard preceding correct reasoning steps, introducing significant noise'. 10K pairs, <500 steps, ~+3% MATH at 70B.
  - relevance: Archetype of the family: shared prefix REQUIRED (prefix-conditioned pair), rejected gradient masked to exactly the located step, localization by GPT-4/human (violates self-contained constraint). Did NOT use verifier-only or self-localization, did not handle off-policy/rescued samples, and it is precisely the prefix-cut pair construction the researcher abandoned. Its observation that errors appear midway matches M4 (failures' privileged disagreement concentrates in the last third).
- **Step-Controlled DPO (SCDPO): Leveraging Stepwise Errors for Enhancing Mathematical Reasoning** — arXiv:2407.00782 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Localization by CONSTRUCTION instead of detection: take a correct solution, pick a random step k, keep steps 1..k verbatim, then continue sampling at elevated temperature (1.1 rising by 0.05 to 1.4) until the final answer is wrong. Error correctness is judged by final answer only (they argue right-answer-wrong-steps is ~0.5%). Loss = L_naive (full-sequence DPO) + L_SC, where L_SC is DPO applied 'only to the steps after t_k', so the rejected-side gradient is restricted to the post-divergence suffix. Equation 6 shows L_SC decomposes into a sum of per-step rewards concentrated where temperature inflated the error rate. InternLM2-20B: 88.5 GSM8K / 58.1 MATH.
  - relevance: Shared prefix required by design; rejected gradient masked to suffix after k; verifier-only signal (self-contained). Closest self-contained version of the step-localized negative, but it manufactures failures from successes rather than localizing the policy's own failures, and is a prefix-conditioned pair (abandoned anchor methodology). Did not study off-policy samples or long CoT (~5k tokens).
- **Self-Explore: Enhancing Mathematical Reasoning in Language Models with Fine-grained Rewards** — arXiv:2404.10346 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: The model itself locates the 'first pit': for each step prefix of a failed rationale, sample k=4 completions at T=0.7 with the RFT model; the first step from which none of the 4 completions reaches the correct answer is the pit. Pair: input = question + all steps before the pit; rejected = the pit step only; chosen = one correct completion from the step before the pit. Objective is unchanged DPO (Eq. 2) applied to these step-granular pairs; 'outcome-level DPO penalizes entire rejected sequences, while step-level DPO penalizes only the first pit'. Ablation on DeepSeek-Math: outcome-pairwise 74.83/34.92 vs granular 78.47/37.68 (GSM8K/MATH).
  - relevance: Self-contained (no judge; model + verifier) localization via cheap-ish MC rollouts (4 per step) — a lighter form of the outcome-MC credit the researcher rejected; requires a shared prefix and masks the rejected gradient to one step. Gives the cleanest published number for 'localized rejected step vs whole-sequence rejected' (+2.8 to +3.6). Did not test long CoT, off-policy/rescued samples, or a privileged teacher.
- **Critical Tokens Matter: Token-Level Contrastive Estimation Enhances LLM's Reasoning Capability (cDPO)** — arXiv:2411.19943 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Critical tokens are defined by rollouts: on an incorrect trajectory, roll out 64 completions from each token position; a token is critical if its correctness is 0 and all later positions stay below 5%. To avoid that cost, contrastive estimation: fine-tune a positive model on correct trajectories and a negative model on frequent incorrect ones, score log s_t = (1+beta) log P^p(y_t) - beta log P^n(y_t) - log Z (beta=1); low s_t = critical. cDPO weights ONLY the rejected side: phi_s(x,y^n) = gamma * sum_t (1 - s_t) log pi_theta(y_t)/pi_ref(y_t), loss = -log sigma(phi(y^p) - phi_s(y^n, s)). No shared prefix needed. GSM8K avg 77.2 vs DPO 70.5 / RPO 75.4; MATH500 33.4 vs 29.4 / 32.3. Contrastive estimation costs 0.002% of rollout cost.
  - relevance: Closest published analog to the researcher's M1/M3 adapter-contrast weighting, but with a different contrast: success-model vs failure-model (not adapter vs base). M2 showed adapter-vs-base g_t does not separate successes from failures; cDPO's pos-vs-neg log-ratio is the signal that would need to be measured. Rejected-side-only token weighting is exactly their design choice. Did not use a privileged teacher, off-policy samples, or long CoT; their critical tokens are content tokens found by rollouts, whereas M1's top-g tokens are style tokens.
- **Token-level Direct Preference Optimization (TDPO)** — arXiv:2404.11999 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Rewrites DPO in a token-level MDP and adds a sequential forward KL term D_SeqKL(x,y) = sum_t KL(pi_ref(.|x,y<t) || pi_theta(.|x,y<t)). TDPO1: -log sigma(u - delta) with u the usual sequence log-ratio margin and delta = beta D_SeqKL(y_l) - beta D_SeqKL(y_w). TDPO2: -log sigma(u - alpha*delta_2) with delta_2 = beta D_SeqKL(y_l) - sg(beta D_SeqKL(y_w)), stop-gradient on the chosen KL so the regularizer actively suppresses KL growth only on the rejected sequence. Signal: response-level preference only; all tokens participate; no shared prefix needed.
  - relevance: Token-level in form but not localized: every rejected token gets gradient; the only asymmetry is the per-token KL control on the rejected side. Evaluated on sentiment/dialogue, not reasoning. Relevant mainly as the formal basis that later token-weighted DPO variants (TIS-DPO, SePO, TGDPO) build on.
- **Focused-DPO: Enhancing Code Generation Through Focus on Critical Points** — arXiv:2502.11475 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Error-Point Identification: sample k=10 codes + tests from the policy, PageRank-rank by mutual test passing, then for each correct/incorrect pair compute the common prefix and common suffix, decomposing into prefix / mid_chosen vs mid_rej / suffix, with a Diff objective that maximizes the middle difference and penalizes long common parts. Loss: chosen reward = prefix ratio + w_focused * mid ratio + suffix ratio; rejected reward = prefix ratio + w_focused * mid ratio with the suffix EXCLUDED; w_focused = 2 amplifies the divergent span (ablations w=1,3,5). No ablation vs plain full-sequence DPO.
  - relevance: Localization is purely textual (longest common prefix/suffix between a correct and an incorrect sample) and self-contained; the rejected gradient is concentrated on the divergent middle and removed from the shared suffix. Requires pairs that share long common prefixes/suffixes — realistic for code, not for 5k-token sampled math CoTs where M2 shows no positional alignment. Did not study reasoning CoT or off-policy samples.
- **SePO: Selective Preference Optimization via Token-Level Reward Function Estimation** — arXiv:2408.13518 (2024 (EMNLP 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Train a small oracle model with DPO, read off token rewards s(y_i) = log pi_ora(y_i|q,y<i)/pi_ref(y_i|q,y<i). Select tokens asymmetrically: top-k% reward tokens in the chosen response, bottom-k% in the rejected response. Optimize a reference-free contrastive objective only on selected tokens: L = -log sigma(u_hat(q,y_w,I_kw) - u_hat(q,y_l,I_kl) - lambda) with u_hat a length-normalized log-likelihood over selected tokens. Performance rises sharply from 10% to 50% selection then plateaus; 30% tokens beats full-token baselines; weak-to-strong (oracle 16.8x smaller).
  - relevance: Direct precedent for masking the rejected-side gradient to a scored subset of tokens, with the score from an implicit reward model — which M5 found to be diffuse/uninformative when read off the stage-2 DPO adapter. No shared prefix; no reasoning domain; their oracle is trained on preference data, not outcome labels. The researcher's 'rescaled C_r * g_t^+' weighting (M3) is an SePO-like selection; SePO's finding that selection below ~30% degrades is a warning against very sparse masks.
- **TIS-DPO: Token-level Importance Sampling for Direct Preference Optimization** — arXiv:2410.04350 (2024 (ICLR 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Premise: ideal DPO data has equal expected reward for every token in the winner and loser, so real data should be importance-weighted per token. Weight w_t = k * exp(mu * clamp(log pi^+(y_t)/pi^-(y_t), L, U)) from a pair of contrastive LLMs; mu > 0 on the chosen response (high-reward tokens up-weighted) and mu < 0 on the rejected (high-reward tokens DOWN-weighted, so the negative gradient lands on low-reward tokens). Loss = -log sigma(u(.,w^w,w^l) - eta(.,w^l)) with a weighted sequential KL on the loser. Three estimators for pi^+/pi^-: (P) the same model under contrastive prompts, (S) separate SFT models on winners/losers, (D) forward/reverse DPO; (D) worked best.
  - relevance: The 'contrastive prompts' estimator is the nearest published relative of the researcher's privileged contrast g'_t (same base model, different prompt) — but they use good/bad instructions, not a gold solution, and the paper is on safety/summarization. Rejected-side weights are localized by a learned/prompted token score, no shared prefix. Did not test reasoning, long CoT, or off-policy samples; M2 suggests the (S)-style estimator built from adapters is non-discriminative here.
- **SparsePO: Controlling Preference Alignment of LLMs via Sparse Token Masks** — arXiv:2410.05102 (2024 (EMNLP 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Learns per-token weight masks that scale both the reward (log-ratio) and the KL contribution of each token in the DPO objective; masks are either derived from the reference model or learned on the fly with a sparsity-inducing term, so most tokens get ~zero weight and a few carry the preference signal. Separate masks can be applied to chosen and rejected responses. +10% / +3% win-rate in summarization/dialogue without hurting reasoning benchmarks.
  - relevance: Published form of 'learn which rejected tokens to penalize' with no external signal; no shared prefix. Not evaluated as a reasoning objective; learned masks need many pairs, which the 1.6% cliff slice does not supply. Did not address off-policy or verifier-graded data.
- **TGDPO: Harnessing Token-Level Reward Guidance for Enhancing Direct Preference Optimization** — arXiv:2506.14574 (2025 (ICML 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Decomposes sequence-level PPO into a chain of token-level PPO problems with token-level reward guidance, derives the closed-form token-level optimal policy and corresponding token reward, then plugs them into Bradley-Terry to get a computable DPO-style loss in which 'different tokens exhibit varying degrees of deviation from reference policy based on their respective rewards'. Guidance comes from an external token-level reward model. Gains of up to 7.5 MT-Bench / 6.2 AlpacaEval2 / 4.3 Arena-Hard over DPO/SimPO.
  - relevance: Shows how an arbitrary per-token signal can be folded into DPO in a principled way (per-token beta), not localization per se; requires an external token reward model (not self-contained). No reasoning experiments, no shared prefix, no off-policy treatment.
- **Full-Step-DPO: Self-Supervised Preference Optimization with Step-wise Rewards for Mathematical Reasoning** — arXiv:2502.14356 (2025 (ACL Findings)) ✓
  - mechanism: Replaces Step-DPO's single located step with rewards on every step from a self-supervised PRM: sample M=32 solutions per problem, label each step of a solution with the solution's final-answer correctness (MC with N=1, O(M) instead of O(MNK)), train the PRM. Pairs = top-4 vs bottom-4 solutions by PRM score (T^2 pairs), no shared prefix. Step-wise DPO: gradient of each step's log-ratio is weighted alpha_i = softmax(gamma r_si) over steps of the chosen solution and softmax(-gamma r_si) over steps of the rejected, so low-reward rejected steps receive the strongest descent. 1.5-3.7% over baselines vs ~1% for Step-DPO; Step-DPO often degraded OOD.
  - relevance: Self-contained alternative to GPT-4 localization: soft, step-level weighting of the rejected side from an outcome-trained PRM; no shared prefix needed. Their 'N=1' PRM label is essentially outcome-as-step-label, which the Qwen PRM paper shows mislabels steps; but for long sampled CoTs it is the cheapest step-localizer. Did not use a privileged teacher or off-policy rescued samples.
- **Step-KTO: Optimizing Mathematical Reasoning through Stepwise Binary Feedback** — arXiv:2501.10799 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: L = L_KTO (outcome-level KTO on final-answer correctness) + L_step (KTO-style value function on each step's implied reward r(x,s_h) = log pi_theta(s_h|x,s_<h)/pi_ref(s_h|x,s_<h), with step-specific lambda_D/lambda_U/beta). Step labels come from prompting Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as a PRM with a structured template. Unpaired (KTO), so no shared prefix; wrong steps get a localized 'undesirable' gradient. MATH-500: 63.2 vs 60.6 KTO at 8B.
  - relevance: Unpaired step-localized negative gradient — attractive because rescued cliffs have 2-3 successes vs 20 failures and no natural pairs — but the localizer is an external 70B judge (violates self-contained). Did not study which steps of long CoTs carry the signal or off-policy samples.
- **Neural Text Generation with Unlikelihood Training** — arXiv:1908.04319 (2019) ✓
  - mechanism: Token-level unlikelihood adds -sum_{c in C_t} log(1 - p(c | x_<t)) for a set of negative candidate tokens C_t (previous context tokens, to fight repetition) on top of MLE; sequence-level unlikelihood applies the same penalty to tokens of model-generated continuations that exhibit the undesired pattern (repeats). Negative candidates are defined by a rule, not a learned signal.
  - relevance: The primitive underneath NSR/UFT/W-REINFORCE: push down p(token) at specific positions. Original use has rule-based candidate sets and no reasoning verifier; the 'which tokens' question is exactly what this family tries to answer with PRMs, rollouts, contrast models, or prompts.
- **Tapered Off-Policy REINFORCE (TOPR): Stable and Efficient Reinforcement Learning for LLMs** — arXiv:2503.14286 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Asymmetric, tapered importance sampling at the SEQUENCE level: for positive-reward trajectories the ratio is set to 1 (a+=b+=1, i.e. plain SFT on successes), for negative-reward trajectories the ratio pi(tau)/mu(tau) is truncated to [0,1] (a-=0, b-=1). No KL term. The REINFORCE baseline c changes the effective proportion of positives p~ = p(1-c)/[1+(1-2p)c]; optimal effective positive fraction ~10-20% regardless of true composition. No token-level localization. 79.6% GSM8K pass@1 off-policy; 8B matches 70B after iterations.
  - relevance: Directly relevant to rescued samples: under TOPR, an adapter-sampled FAILURE has sequence ratio pi_base/pi_adapter = exp(-63..-200) ~ 0, so its negative gradient is tapered to nothing — the principled off-policy view says adapter failures should not be used as negatives at all, consistent with LSPO discarding failed re-rollouts; only the base policy's own 8/8 failures are on-policy negatives. TOPR did not localize negatives within a sequence and did not use a privileged teacher.
- **The Surprising Effectiveness of Negative Reinforcement in LLM Reasoning (NSR, W-REINFORCE)** — arXiv:2506.01347 (2025 (NeurIPS 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Decomposes REINFORCE with verifier reward into PSR (maximize log pi of correct responses) and NSR (minimize log pi of incorrect: -E[sum_{y: r=-1} -pi_theta(y|x)] style objective). W-REINFORCE = lambda * PSR + NSR with lambda = 0.1. Token-level gradient analysis: for the sampled wrong token v=y_t the logit gradient is -pi_v(1-pi_v) (weaker when the model is confident), and for unsampled tokens +pi_{y_t} pi_v, i.e. mass is redistributed proportionally to the model's prior — so NSR refines without collapsing diversity. Uniform over all tokens of the failed response; no localization. Qwen3-4B non-thinking: NSR reaches MATH Pass@64 98.0, near thinking mode; PSR-only fails to activate latent capability and hurts pass@k.
  - relevance: Same base family (Qwen3-4B) and verifier-only signal; argues negatives mainly redistribute mass toward the model's own plausible alternatives, which is exactly what is wanted on cliffs where the base's 8/8 failures are on-policy. But NSR works because its negatives are ON-policy; the researcher's rescued failures are adapter-sampled (off-policy), so the paper's mechanism does not transfer directly. No per-token localization, no shared prefix, no privileged teacher.
- **Asymmetric REINFORCE for off-Policy Reinforcement Learning (AsymRE)** — arXiv:2506.20520 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Off-policy REINFORCE with advantage A = r - V where V is a tunable baseline rather than the expected reward; shows theoretically (bandit) and empirically (LLM reasoning) that off-policy updates should put more weight on positive-reward samples than on negative ones, with a policy-improvement guarantee when V lower-bounds the expected reward. Sequence-level, no importance ratios needed, no token localization.
  - relevance: Second independent argument (with TOPR) that negative gradient from off-policy samples should be damped; for rescued adapter samples this means successes are the safe signal and failures are not. Did not address where within a sequence the negative should go, nor rescued/scaffolded data.
- **Bridging Supervised Learning and Reinforcement Learning in Math Reasoning (NFT, negative-aware fine-tuning)** — arXiv:2505.18116 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Keeps the SFT loss on self-generated correct answers and, instead of discarding incorrect ones, constructs an implicit negative policy from the same parameters (p_neg proportional to the positive policy's residual mass) so that maximizing likelihood of negatives under the implicit negative policy becomes a gradient that pushes them down; proves equivalence to GRPO in strict on-policy training. Sequence-level; no token localization. 7B/32B: beats RFT, matches GRPO/DAPO.
  - relevance: Shows the student can absorb verifier-graded failures inside an SFT-shaped objective (fits the WeightedSFTTrainer path better than trl DPO), but again assumes on-policy negatives and does not localize. Did not consider off-policy rescued trajectories.
- **Offline Learning and Forgetting for Reasoning with Large Language Models (UFT)** — arXiv:2504.11364 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Unlikelihood Fine-Tuning: J = (1-alpha) J_NLL(D+) + alpha J_UL(D-), NLL on successful search paths plus a token-level unlikelihood term on failed paths with alpha near 0 (auxiliary). Failed paths are labelled by a rule-based verifier; explicitly 'unlike prior work that identify high-credit steps with more computation, we sidestep this with an auxiliary loss without preference pairs'. Outperforms SFT, SimPO, CPO-SimPO on Countdown/Sudoku.
  - relevance: The unpaired, verifier-only, SFT-native way to add a negative term — one line in WeightedSFTTrainer (region weight < 0 on failure tokens) — with the explicit choice NOT to localize. Did not study off-policy failures, long CoT, or token credit.
- **On the Effect of Negative Gradient in Group Relative Deep Reinforcement Optimization (Lazy Likelihood Displacement, NTHR)** — arXiv:2505.18830 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Finds Lazy Likelihood Displacement: under GRPO the likelihood of correct responses rises only marginally or falls, because all tokens of incorrect responses are penalized uniformly and tokens whose hidden states are similar to tokens of correct responses drag those down (gradient coupling through hidden-state alignment). NTHR (Negative Token Hidden Reward) computes, per token of a negative sample, its coupling to the positive samples via hidden-embedding similarity and scales that token's negative advantage by eta, i.e. downweights the penalty on tokens shared with correct reasoning. Small gains on 0.5B-3B Qwen-Math (41.14 -> 41.94).
  - relevance: Directly addresses the researcher's situation: M2 shows rescued successes and failures have identical token profiles, so uniform negative gradient on failures would displace the successes (consistent with M5's neutral DPO adapter). NTHR is a self-contained token-level PROTECTION mask for the rejected side (no localizer needed); a follow-up (arXiv:2509.23870) reports it fails in agentic tasks because the protected tokens are the reasoning-critical ones. Did not use a teacher or off-policy samples.
- **ResRL: Boosting LLM Reasoning via Negative Sample Projection Residual Reinforcement Learning** — arXiv:2605.00380 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Builds on LLD: projects each negative-token hidden representation onto an SVD low-rank subspace spanned by positive-sample representations and uses the projection residual to modulate that token's negative gradient — tokens aligned with the positive subspace get little penalty, orthogonal ones keep full penalty. Derives a single-forward proxy upper-bounding representation alignment for conservative advantage reweighting. +9.4 Avg@16 / +7.0 Pass@128 over NSR-style baselines across 12 benchmarks.
  - relevance: Token-level localization of the negative gradient by geometry relative to successes, self-contained, no pairs/prefix. Evaluated on on-policy RL; not on off-policy rescued data or with a gold teacher.
- **GTPO and GRPO-S: Token and Sequence-Level Reward Shaping with Policy Entropy** — arXiv:2508.04349 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Redistributes the sequence reward across tokens by policy entropy: for successful sequences r~_{i,t}^+ = alpha1 r_i + alpha2 (H_{i,t}/sum_k H_{k,t}) d_t (bonus on high-entropy tokens); for failed sequences confident (low-entropy) wrong tokens are penalized more heavily; continuous rather than DAPO's binary entropy mask; reward conservation when alpha1+alpha2=1. Also protects 'conflict' tokens shared between positive and negative group members. +29.4 Pass@32 AIME24 on Qwen2.5-7B vs GRPO/DAPO.
  - relevance: A self-contained per-token reshaping of the negative side using only the policy's entropy — directly testable with M7 statistics (mean entropy 0.28) — but its prescription (penalize confident tokens on failures) is the opposite of HAPO's (put negative credit on high-entropy tokens), so the family has no consensus. On-policy only; no teacher.
- **Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective RL for LLM Reasoning** — arXiv:2506.01939 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Masks the policy-gradient (both positive and negative advantage) to the top-20% highest-entropy tokens per batch; these 'forking' tokens are spread throughout the chain. Training on the 80% low-entropy tokens degrades; 20% matches or exceeds full-gradient, with gains growing with scale (Qwen3-32B +11 AIME25).
  - relevance: Localizes BOTH signs of gradient by a self-contained criterion with no localizer; M1/M7 (Jaccard(g, entropy)=0.33; entropy top-20% overlaps surprisal top-20% 0.75) already characterize this mask on rescued samples. Did not separate where negatives should go vs positives, nor off-policy samples.
- **Where Hindsight Credit Can Reside: A Signed-Capacity View of Token Updates in RLVR (HAPO)** — arXiv:2604.11056 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Four-quadrant decomposition of token updates by reward polarity x token entropy; argues high-entropy tokens have capacity to absorb both positive and negative hindsight credit while low-entropy updates saturate quickly, and that sustained gains concentrate in the signed high-entropy quadrants. HAPO is a sign-preserving GRPO modification that reallocates advantages toward tokens with capacity. Competitive with entropy-aware baselines on math.
  - relevance: Offers a principle for WHERE the negative gradient can land at all (high-entropy tokens) — relevant because M1 found 76-78% of SFT loss sits on tokens both policies are unsure about. Self-contained; no teacher or off-policy analysis.
- **Credit Assignment with Resets in Language Model Reasoning (SRPO, Self-Reset Policy Optimization)** — arXiv:2605.25507 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: The same policy that produced a failed trace is prompted with its own labelled thoughts and returns the index of the first incorrect thought (explicit self-localization, no external supervision). From that reset state it samples ~4 suffix continuations; a second group-relative advantage is computed within the shared-prefix group, and the shared-prefix loss applies the advantage only to suffix tokens: L_SP = -(1/G) sum_i (1/T_i) sum_t A^i log pi(y_{i,t}|x*, y_{i,<t}) with prefix tokens masked. Framed as conservative policy iteration with a credit-assignment oracle that targets improvable states. Beats GRPO/RRPO, 2-3x faster on code; clean reset points succeed 28.7% vs 16.3% for wrong ones, so localization quality is the bottleneck.
  - relevance: The only self-contained 'first wrong step' localizer in the family (model critiques itself; verifier judges suffixes), and it quantifies how much a correct localization is worth. But it is a shared-prefix/reset method (prefix-conditioned continuation = the abandoned anchor methodology) and it relies on resampling from the reset, which is the MC-rollout cost the researcher rejected. No gold-solution teacher; not tested on cliff (0/8) prompts where suffix sampling from the base still fails.
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search Boosts Reasoning via Iterative Preference Learning (MCTS-DPO)** — arXiv:2405.00451 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: MCTS over reasoning steps with shared prefixes; Q-values for candidate steps from rollouts plus stepwise self-evaluation; sibling steps with high vs low Q become step-level DPO pairs (chosen/rejected share the prefix, gradient on the step). Iterated online. Mistral-7B: GSM8K 81.8 (+5.9), MATH 34.7 (+5.8).
  - relevance: Step-localized negative via shared-prefix tree search; localization by MC rollouts (rejected as too heavy) plus self-evaluation. Same shape as SVPO (arXiv:2406.10858) and AlphaMath. Not applicable under the no-anchor constraint; no teacher/off-policy treatment.
- **Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations** — arXiv:2312.08935 (2023) ✓
  - mechanism: Step labels by MC completion from each step (hard label = any completion correct; soft label = fraction correct); trains a PRM used for reranking and for step-by-step PPO rewards. Mistral-7B GSM8K 77.9 -> 84.1 (PPO) -> 89.1 (verification).
  - relevance: The canonical MC-rollout localizer; OmegaPRM (arXiv:2406.06592) makes it a binary search over the chain to find the first error; PAV (arXiv:2410.08146) turns it into per-step advantages under a prover policy; VinePPO (arXiv:2410.01679) does it online per step. The Qwen PRM lessons paper (arXiv:2501.07301) shows MC step labels are unreliable (right answer, wrong steps) and that PRMs drift to scoring the final-answer step — a caution for any MC-derived token weights.
- **ProcessBench: Identifying Process Errors in Mathematical Reasoning** — arXiv:2412.06559 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Benchmark of 3,400 competition/Olympiad solutions with the earliest erroneous step annotated by human experts; models must output the first wrong step or 'all correct'. MC-trained PRMs fail to generalize beyond GSM8K/MATH and underperform prompted critic models (QwQ-32B ~ GPT-4o < o1-mini); a simply fine-tuned PRM does better than MC-label PRMs.
  - relevance: Ground truth on how hard self-contained first-error localization is: small models prompted to find the error (as SRPO does) are weak, and BIG-Bench Mistake (arXiv:2311.08516) found LLMs 'cannot find reasoning errors but can correct them given the location'. FoVer (arXiv:2505.15960) gets step labels from formal verifiers (Z3/Isabelle) with no LLM calls — the Lean path is the only fully self-contained step-level oracle in this literature. None of these use a gold-solution-conditioned teacher as the localizer.
- **Process Reinforcement through Implicit Rewards (PRIME)** — arXiv:2502.01456 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Implicit PRM: token reward r_t = beta log pi_phi(y_t|.)/pi_ref(y_t|.) from a model pi_phi trained online with a cross-entropy outcome objective on policy rollouts; token rewards are summed into advantages and combined with outcome advantage for RLOO-style updates. Eurus-2-7B-PRIME +15.1% avg over SFT. Builds on 'From r to Q*' (arXiv:2404.12358), which argues DPO's beta log-ratio is an implicit per-token credit, and RTO (arXiv:2404.18922), which feeds DPO token rewards into PPO.
  - relevance: This is the 'implicit PRM read off a trained log-ratio' that M5 tested on the stage-2 DPO adapter and found diffuse (paired 30/57). PRIME's version is trained online on many rollouts with outcome labels, not a 4-step LoRA, so M5 does not refute PRIME but shows the adapter-based shortcut does not produce a usable token signal. No localization of the negative side beyond the learned token rewards; no gold teacher.
- **Exploring the Limit of Outcome Reward for Learning Mathematical Reasoning (OREAL)** — arXiv:2502.06781 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Positives: behavior cloning on best-of-N correct trajectories (L1 = -log pi(s) + beta KL). Negatives: reward reshaped by (1-p) with p the success rate to keep gradient consistency. Token credit: a lightweight token reward model w(s<=t) trained with cross-entropy on binary outcome labels; per-token importance omega+ = max(2 sigma(w)-1, 0) on positive samples and omega- = max(1 - 2 sigma(w), 0) on negative samples, so negative gradient concentrates on tokens the credit model flags as failure-predictive. OREAL-7B 91.0 MATH-500, 32B 95.0.
  - relevance: Self-contained (outcome labels only) token-level localization of the negative side via a learned credit model, plus an explicit success-rate reshaping for negatives — a fully published version of 'weight failure tokens by a learned failure predictor'. Needs enough outcome-labelled rollouts to train w; not designed for off-policy rescued samples or a gold teacher.
- **Unintentional Unalignment: Likelihood Displacement in Direct Preference Optimization** — arXiv:2410.08847 (2024) ✓
  - mechanism: Proves and measures that DPO decreases the likelihood of the preferred response when preferred and dispreferred are similar, via the centered hidden embedding similarity (CHES) score; mass flows to unrelated outputs (preferring 'No' over 'Never' raises 'Yes'; refusal rate 74.4 -> 33.4). Fix: filter pairs with high CHES. Companion results: DPO-Positive (arXiv:2402.13228) adds a hinge that forbids chosen-likelihood decrease on low-edit-distance math pairs; Gradient Entanglement (arXiv:2410.13828) shows the failure is governed by the inner product of chosen/rejected log-prob gradients; DaGRPO (arXiv:2512.06337) masks whole low-distinctiveness sample pairs in GRPO.
  - relevance: Explains why pairwise negative objectives on rescued samples are risky: M2 shows successes and failures from the same adapter are token-profile-identical (maximal CHES), and M5's DPO adapter separated its training pairs yet was neutral on conversion. Any negative-side objective here needs a displacement guard (DPOP hinge, NTHR/ResRL protection, or CHES-style pair filtering). None of these papers considered rescued/off-policy data.
- **Towards Widening the Distillation Bottleneck for Reasoning Models (Marco-o1 v2)** — arXiv:2503.01461 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: MCTS with rule-based correctness generates long CoT; for DPO, negatives are incorrect paths sharing a MINIMAL prefix with the positive to reduce token overlap, and the DPO loss mask zeroes the common-prefix tokens 'analogous to the treatment of padding tokens' so only divergent segments contribute gradient. Reported cumulatively (GSM8K 87.2 / MATH 51.0 vs 86.2 / 41.8), masking not isolated.
  - relevance: The simplest published 'mask the shared prefix, penalize only the divergent suffix' DPO for long CoT, self-contained; requires tree-structured pairs sharing a prefix (anchor-like). Did not localize within the suffix or handle off-policy samples.
- **Earlier Tokens Contribute More: Learning Direct Preference Optimization From Temporal Decay Perspective (D2PO)** — arXiv:2502.14340 (2025 (ICLR 2025)) ✓
  - mechanism: Shows DPO's uniform token weighting induces length bias and over-weights late tokens; adds a temporal decay gamma^t on each token's log-ratio contribution (both sides) so earlier tokens dominate the preference margin. +5.9-8.8 AlpacaEval2, +3.3-9.7 Arena-Hard, no loss on GSM8K/MATH.
  - relevance: A position-only localization prior; contradicts what M4 finds on failures (disagreement mass concentrates in the LAST third), so a decay prior would point the negative gradient away from the wrong turn on cliff failures. CPPO (arXiv:2606.10968) similarly tightens trust regions on early tokens and relaxes late ones with a cumulative prefix budget (+5.6 AIME avg@16 on Qwen3-30B-A3B). Neither uses verifier-located errors or off-policy data.
- **ConfPO: Exploiting Policy Model Confidence for Critical Token Selection in Preference Optimization** — arXiv:2506.08712 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Selects preference-critical tokens using only the training policy's own confidence (no auxiliary model or annotator), and concentrates the DAA (DPO/SimPO-style) update and its KL budget on those tokens rather than all tokens; zero extra compute. Outperforms uniform DAAs on AlpacaEval 2 / Arena-Hard.
  - relevance: Self-contained token selection from the student's own probabilities — the same raw statistic as M7 (surprisal/entropy) and the C(y) learnability score; not tested on reasoning or on the rejected side specifically. Did not use a teacher or off-policy samples.
- **STAPO: Stabilizing Reinforcement Learning for LLMs by Silencing Rare Spurious Tokens** — arXiv:2602.15620 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Identifies ~0.01% 'spurious' tokens with low probability, low entropy and positive advantage that inherit the full sequence reward and get abnormally amplified gradients; S2T silences their gradient. +7.13 / +3.69 avg over GRPO, 20-Entropy, JustRL on six math benchmarks (Qwen 1.7B-14B).
  - relevance: A positive-side analog of localization (mask, not weight) chosen by the student's own statistics; relevant because rescued successes are low-probability under the base (sequence log-ratio +63..+200) and STAPO warns that low-prob, low-entropy tokens in positive samples destabilize training. Not about negatives, teacher, or off-policy.
- **Beyond Entropy: Learning from Token-Level Distributional Deviations for LLM Reasoning (ICT)** — arXiv:2606.19771 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Identifies critical branching tokens by the Jensen-Shannon divergence of a token's logit distribution from the typical distribution (rather than entropy alone) and restricts policy-gradient updates to the top-10% most distributionally unique tokens; avg +4.58 pass@4 (max +14.9) over GRPO, 20-Entropy and STAPO on Qwen2.5 0.5B-7B.
  - relevance: Another self-contained token-selection rule for both signs of gradient that could be read off the base model on rescued samples; not specific to negatives and not tested off-policy.
- **Not All Negative Samples Are Equal: LLMs Learn Better from Plausible Reasoning** — arXiv:2602.03516 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Synthesizes 'plausible negative samples' by reverse RL with a composite reward (format, accuracy inversion, RM score, CoT validity), then uses them as DPO rejected responses against the model's own verifier-correct samples. Plausible negatives' reward distribution is close to positives (W-distance 1.12 vs 3.70 for rejection-sampled negatives) and their errors are 'knowledge errors' preserving logical structure; +2.03% avg, +33% relative AIME24 on Qwen2.5-7B.
  - relevance: Evidence that the VALUE of a negative depends on how close it is to the success distribution — adapter-sampled failures on cliffs are exactly such near-miss negatives — but obtained with an external reward model (not self-contained) and without token localization. Also note LSPO and TOPR argue the opposite for off-policy negatives.
- **Turning Dust into Gold: Distilling Complex Reasoning Capabilities from LLMs by Leveraging Negative Data** — arXiv:2312.12832 (2024 (AAAI)) ✓
  - mechanism: Three-stage framework using wrong teacher rationales: NAT (negative-assisted training) trains separate LoRA experts on positive and negative rationales and mixes them; NCE (negative-calibrated enhancement) adds a KL term that calibrates the student against the negative expert on samples where it disagrees with the positive one (from memory; the abstract fetched confirms only the three-stage negative-data framework and ASC as a ranking model reweighting self-consistency candidates). Sequence-level; no token localization.
  - relevance: Early evidence that negatives help distillation, but via model mixing rather than token-level negative gradient; no verifier-localized error and no off-policy treatment. Learning From Failure for agents (arXiv:2402.11651) similarly uses conditional SFT with a good/bad prefix tag rather than unlikelihood.
- **LoRA Scaffolded Policy Optimization (LSPO)** — arXiv:2607.27787 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Each RL step: detect cliff prompts (all rollouts fail), fit a transient LoRA by SFT on ground-truth solutions for those prompts, re-roll with base+adapter, and splice ONLY the successful re-rollouts into the GRPO batch in place of failed base rollouts ('a failed re-rollout carries no signal the group does not already have... admitting it would add off-policy mass without adding information'). Correction is the standard per-token clipped ratio rho_t = pi_theta(y_t)/pi_prev(y_t) with pi_prev = base+adapter for spliced rows; advantages recomputed on the spliced batch; the adapter gets SFT gradients only and is discarded. No ablations on the IS correction, rank, or splice variants. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B on DeepMath: AIME24 pass@4 +10.7, avg +3.8 over 16 benchmarks.
  - relevance: The scaffold mechanism the researcher adopts; its learning rule is the thing to beat. It deliberately discards adapter failures (no negative side at all on cliffs) and M6 shows its per-token IS correction is inert (92-96% of tokens inside the clip) — so LSPO's student-side objective is effectively uniform on-policy-style PG on rescued successes. It leaves open exactly the question in scope: whether and where a negative/rejected-side signal should be applied to rescued trajectories.
- **Learning to Reason under Off-Policy Guidance (LUFFY)** — arXiv:2504.14945 (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Mixed-policy GRPO: off-policy expert traces (DeepSeek-R1) are added to each group, advantages normalized over the union of on- and off-policy rewards; the off-policy importance ratio pi_theta/pi_phi sets pi_phi = 1; policy shaping replaces the ratio with f(r) = r/(r+gamma), gamma = 0.1, which amplifies gradient on tokens the student assigns LOW probability (the novel reasoning moves) to prevent superficial imitation and entropy collapse. Expert traces are positive-advantage by construction; no negative side from off-policy data. +6.4 avg on six math benchmarks; trains Llama-3.1-8B where on-policy RLVR fails.
  - relevance: Nearest published answer to 'how should a student learn off-policy successes': positive-only, with a per-token shaping that emphasizes low-probability tokens — relevant to M1's finding that 76-78% of loss sits on tokens both policies are unsure about. Does not use off-policy failures as negatives (consistent with TOPR/AsymRE/LSPO); no localization of negatives; expert is an external model, not a self-scaffold.

## Family: LSPO / HDPO / privileged-guidance RL (38 papers)

### Scout synthesis

WHAT LSPO/HDPO ACTUALLY DO WITH RESCUED TRAJECTORIES. LSPO's student objective is nothing more than DAPO-GRPO: the verifier-passing adapter re-rolls are swapped into the failed slots of their own group, advantages are recomputed (so the spliced success is the +1 and the remaining base failures are the negatives), and the only 'correction' is the ordinary per-token clipped PPO ratio with the adapter log-prob in the denominator (eps 0.20/0.28). There is no per-token credit, no sequence-level weight, no quality score, failed re-rolls are thrown away, and the author states the estimator is NOT an unbiased sequence-level one and defers 'formal characterization' to future work; the limitations list adapter rank / SFT steps / splice variant / answer-space guessing as unexplored axes and worked-solution dependence. Our M6 (92-96% of tokens inside the clip; gap mass sits where the clip flattens) shows that in our regime this correction is numerically inert, i.e. LSPO's student is effectively on-policy GRPO on a borrowed positive plus the question's own failures. HDPO is the other pole: it never samples from the bare question at all; it distills the privileged self-teacher's top-64 distribution (symmetric JSD, token-averaged, lambda-weighted, separately normalized) into the student along teacher-sampled R=1 'bridge' trajectories, with no weights, no negatives, no IS, and leaves 're-inject converted cliffs into RL' as future work. No paper cites LSPO yet (3.5 weeks old; S2 citation graph empty). HDPO has ~28 citing papers, almost all privileged-self-distillation variants.

WHICH OF OUR CANDIDATE IDEAS ARE ALREADY PUBLISHED UNDER ANOTHER NAME. (1) 'Privileged contrast g'_t = log p(y_t|x,y*) - log p(y_t|x) as a per-token weight' = RLSD's evidence ratio w_t=(P_T/P_S)^{sign A} clipped (2604.03128), AMR-SD's thresholded CIG (2605.18529), SDPG's positive-gated OPD advantage (2606.04036), OC-GRPO's per-token ratio on both successes and failures (2607.19313), and on the GOLD solution SORT's sqrt(p_base p_plan) weight for zero-reward prompts (2605.11505). (2) 'Adapter contrast g_t = log p_adapter - log p_base as a weight' = CAST's non-privileged self-teacher log-ratio with asymmetric clipping and advantage flipping (2606.00172). (3) 'Verifier decides sign, contrast decides intensity' (what our M2/M4 say is the only defensible use) = SC-GRPO's multiplicative f(KL)=D/(D+c) (2606.18810), RLSD, AMR-SD. (4) 'Sequence-level teacher score contrasted against the question's own failures' (our M3 construction) = GC-OPD's group-z-normalized residual between verifier reward and trajectory OPD score, spread with tanh-bounded relative token advantages (2608.19181) and Uni-OPD's order-consistency calibration. (5) 'Learnability C(y) as a filter' = FiRe-OPD's teacher-likelihood trajectory filter (2606.02684). (6) 'Distill the privileged teacher into the student on bridge trajectories' = HDPO itself (and pi-Distill). (7) 'Use the question's own failures as negatives' exists as teacher-PROMPT contrast (MOPD 2605.12652: y*, success, failure in context; CEPO 2605.19436: correct-answer vs wrong-answer teachers; OGLS-SD 2605.12400: +pool minus -pool logits; ZPPO: failures in prompt) and as group advantage (LSPO), but NOT as DPO on adapter-rescued pairs — our stage-2 DPO adapter has no published twin, though M5 shows it buys nothing. (8) 'Cliff term with its own lambda/normalizer' (the fix for the 1.6%-of-mix detectability problem) = HDPO's lambda L_JSD and SRPO's separately-normalized routed losses (2604.02288).

WHAT THE FAMILY IMPLIES FOR OUR OBJECTIVE. (a) Every per-token privileged/adapter-contrast weighting in this family is used as a MAGNITUDE modulator on a verifier-signed advantage, never as the credit itself; our measurements (g identical on successes vs failures; weights numerically uniform; positive g' non-discriminative) are consistent with that consensus, so a token-weighted SFT on rescued successes is both already-published (SORT/RLSD/CAST) and, by our own M3 falsification, inert — drop it. (b) The family's own diagnostics (Kaur, Kim, Purified OPSD, AD-OPSD) show the privileged teacher's per-token signal lives on epistemic/format tokens at high-entropy forks, which is exactly our M1/M4 token-kind result; the published remedy is to SUBTRACT a reference-only component (Purified OPSD's log pi_T - log pi_ref with a solution-only teacher) or to contrast two conditionings that share style (CEPO, MOPD, OGLS-SD). A self-contained token contrast that is not yet measured in our repo is log p(y_t|x,y*) - log p(y_t|y*) (question-conditioned residual) or log p(y_t|x,y*) - log p(y_t|x,y_wrong) — if either concentrates on math tokens where g and g' did not, that is a new measurement worth one CPU/GPU diagnostic before any training arm. (c) The genuinely open space, untouched by LSPO and HDPO and explicitly deferred by LSPO, is SEQUENCE-level treatment of rescued samples: our M6 shows off-policy-ness is a sequence property, HiLL publishes a bound (p >= p_h exp(-rho_c), rho_c = mean per-token contrast) saying low-reliance rescued trajectories transfer to the bare policy, FiRe-OPD/GC-OPD weight by trajectory-level teacher scores, and M4's negative-disagreement mass already discriminates correct from incorrect at the sequence level (p=6e-4 / 3e-6). A sequence-weighted SFT/rejection rule over rescued successes (weight or select by low privileged-disagreement mass and/or low adapter reliance, inside a separately-normalized cliff loss term with its own lambda) is self-contained, cheap, consistent with all seven measurements, and unpublished for adapter-rescued trajectories in an EI loop. (d) On negatives: the family uses failures either through the group advantage (LSPO: only the base failures, adapter failures discarded) or in the teacher prompt (MOPD/CEPO/OGLS-SD); OGLS-SD and Prefix-Teach/Suffix-Fade show location-aware use (dense signal on failures' prefix, fade the suffix), matching our M4 finding that failures' disagreement concentrates in the last third. If a negative term is kept, the published-consistent form is a LOCALIZED penalty on the wrong-turn window of rescued failures rather than whole-sequence DPO (which M5 shows is diffuse). (e) Transfer to held-out cliffs is untested by LSPO (reports surpass rate, not downstream solvability), HDPO (names cliff re-injection as future work), and every citing paper; it is the cheapest unclaimed result in the family and the one that decides whether any cliff objective matters at 1.6% of the mix.

## Family: Privileged & on-policy self-distillation (41 papers)

### Scout synthesis

1) What the family does. The standard privileged self-distillation objective is: sample y from the student on bare x, score every token with the same model given (x, c) where c is a gold solution, answer, hint, feedback or a verified sibling success, and push the student toward the teacher with either the sampled-token log-ratio as a per-token advantage (SDFT, Thinking Machines, SDPO advantage form, OPPO) or a full/top-K distribution divergence (Self-Distilled Reasoner forward KL, SDPO top-100 JSD, PTD-PO, Qwen3/DeepSeek-V4). Our M4 quantity g'_t = log p_base(y_t|x,y*) - log p_base(y_t|x) is literally the SDFT reward, the SDPO advantage, OPPO's log Bayes factor and CREDIT's r_t, so 'privileged contrast as token weight' is already published under four names. 2) Teacher disagreeing with verified-correct trajectories is a known and documented pathology, not an open question: Rethinking-OPSD-for-Thinking-Models (fork suppression even on correct rollouts, Qwen3-4B included), Why-SD-Degrades (epistemic verbalization suppression), Feedback-Alignment (reference-solution teacher pressures every token because derivations differ stylistically), Sampled-Demonstrations (objective rewards similarity to the demo, collapses diversity), RLCSD (privilege-induced style drift) and Denser-is-not-Better all report what our M1/M4 measured: the privileged signal loads on style/format tokens and punishes correct trajectories whose approach differs from gold. 3) Published remedies fall into four buckets, each mapping to one of our candidate ideas: (a) verifier owns the sign, teacher owns magnitude (OPPO direction anchoring, RLSD, EGRSD, SGSD polarity) = our 'verifier-signed g' weighting'; (b) gate out conflicts (RG-OPD sequence gate requiring teacher likelihood above student on correct rollouts, SG-OPD token sign consistency) = 'drop correct samples the teacher dislikes', which per M4 would discard most rescued successes; (c) baseline the contrast to strip generic/style mass (CREDIT across inputs, RLCSD across correct-vs-wrong hints, OGLS-SD across outcomes, Sibling-Guided credit) = our M3, which failed when baselined across outcomes but has two untested axes (input, hint); (d) distill the privileged teacher ONLY into failures and learn successes by plain or surprisal-weighted MLE (SCOPE, PTD-PO, SSOPD, HSD, AOPD) = our C(y)/class-B weighting for successes plus a failure-side objective. 4) Credit localization without prefix cutting exists: HSD and GEAR localize at the first divergence from a sibling success or at g' spikes; PW-OPSD shows late-position teacher tokens are reliable and early ones are not (AUROC 0.83), which independently explains our M4 location split (successes differ early from gold, failures err late); KAT and Rock-Tokens show near-zero-disagreement regions are inert, which is the state our adapter contrast g is in (M6). 5) What nobody has done, i.e. where novelty is defensible: (i) learning verifier-correct samples that are OFF-policy for the student (adapter-rescued) with a privileged self-teacher; only pi-Distill trains the student on PI-conditioned teacher samples, with an IS weight plus forward KL on agent tasks; (ii) using the ~20 rescued FAILURES per cliff, which STAGED discards, as the distillation target with the rescued successes as the privileged context (the HSD/SSOPD objective on adapter-sampled rollouts) and the gold-conditioned g' negative mass, which M4 shows is a wrong-turn detector concentrated in the last third, as the token mask; (iii) treating g' on successes as a sequence-level quality score for selection or DPO-pair construction rather than as a token weight, since M4 shows it discriminates at the sequence level but not usefully at the token level; (iv) sequence-level rather than per-token off-policy correction for rescued samples (W2SPO tempered coefficient, RG-OPD gate), given M6. Implication for the objective choice: do not build another dense per-token weight on successes from g or g' (already published and empirically inert or style-loaded here); keep surprisal/learnability-weighted SFT on rescued successes (SCOPE path 2, PriFT), and spend the novelty budget on the failure side and on sequence-level selection, where the family's own evidence says the signal lives.

### Papers

- **Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning (SDFT)** — arXiv:2601.19897 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Teacher = the same model conditioned on (x, expert demonstration c); student = same weights conditioned on x only. Student samples its own rollouts and minimizes reverse KL D_KL(pi_theta(.|x) || pi(.|x,c)), implemented with the sampled-token log-ratio r_t = log pi(y_t | x, c, prefix) minus log pi(y_t | x, prefix) as a per-token implicit reward in a policy-gradient estimator (teacher fixed). No verifier, no correctness filtering, every student sample used, uniform over tokens. Qwen2.5-7B: +4 to +9 pts over SFT on new tasks with far less forgetting, about 4x SFT wall-clock.
  - relevance: Their per-token reward is exactly our M4 privileged contrast g'_t (c = gold y*), but applied on-policy to base samples rather than to adapter-rescued off-policy successes. No treatment of the teacher disagreeing with a verified-correct trajectory; continual-learning tasks, not cliff math.
- **Self-Distilled Reasoner: On-Policy Self-Distillation for LLMs (OPSD)** — arXiv:2601.18734 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Single Qwen3 model; teacher sees (x, reference solution y*) with a rationalize instruction, student sees x. Student rollouts (1k-token budget) receive per-token FULL-vocabulary divergence D(p_T || p_S) averaged over length; forward KL is best, the sampled-token policy-gradient variant is consistently worse. No verifier; correct and incorrect rollouts supervised alike. Adds per-token pointwise-KL clipping because stylistic tokens dominated the loss. Qwen3-1.7B 37.1 to 43.4 avg on AIME24/25+HMMT25 vs GRPO 37.7.
  - relevance: Canonical privileged-context OPSD. Their finding that style tokens dominate the privileged signal matches our M1/M4. They did not use a verifier, did not treat correct trajectories differently, and did not study off-policy (adapter-sampled) rollouts.
- **On-Policy Distillation (Thinking Machines Lab blog)** — https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/ (2025) ✓
  - mechanism: Student samples; per-token advantage = negative sampled-token reverse KL, i.e. advantages = -(student_logprob - teacher_logprob), plugged into the standard RL importance-sampling loss. No reward model or verifier. Teacher is a stronger model or an earlier checkpoint of the same model (personalization experiment); they claim high-penalty tokens correspond to forking tokens that led reasoning astray.
  - relevance: Defines the sampled-token log-ratio-as-advantage recipe our adapter contrast g_t would instantiate with teacher = base+LoRA. No privileged context, no verifier, no analysis of whether the log-ratio discriminates correct from incorrect samples (our M2 says it does not for the adapter).
- **Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation (SDPO)** — arXiv:2601.20802 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Loss = sum_t KL(pi_theta(.|x,prefix) || stopgrad(pi_theta(.|x,f,prefix))), full distribution via top-K=100, with per-token advantage A_t = log pi(y_t|x,f,prefix) - log pi(y_t|x,prefix) in a policy-gradient; JSD and EMA/interpolated teacher for stability. Feedback f = runtime errors, test logs, or, in scalar-reward-only environments, a successful sibling rollout from the same batch. Correct rollouts are also distilled (teacher mostly agrees but sharpens). No verifier beyond environment reward; no correctness filtering. Qwen3-8B LiveCodeBench 48.8 vs GRPO 41.2.
  - relevance: Same objective family as our M4, and their scalar-reward fallback (teacher conditioned on a sibling success) is realizable in our setting with rescued successes as the privileged context. They never gate by verifier or localize credit, and never face off-policy rescued samples.
- **Privileged Information Distillation for Language Models (pi-Distill / OPSD)** — arXiv:2602.04942 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Shared-weight teacher (conditioned on PI such as tool-call traces or self-generated hints) and student (unconditioned). pi-Distill: J = alpha*[E R - beta KL(pi_T || sg(pi_S))] + (1-alpha)*[E (pi_S/sg(pi_T)) R - beta KL(sg(pi_T) || pi_S)], i.e. teacher does RL with a reverse-KL tether to the student, student learns from teacher samples with an importance weight and forward KL. OPSD variant: J = E R - beta KL(pi_S || sg(pi_T)) on student samples. Environment scalar reward; no separate verifier. Qwen3-4B/8B on TravelPlanner/tau-bench, up to +11.8 over SFT+RL.
  - relevance: The only paper in this family that trains the student on the PI-conditioned teacher's own samples (off-policy for the student) with an importance weight plus forward KL, structurally the closest published analogue to learning adapter-rescued trajectories. Agent tasks, no math verifier, no token-level credit.
- **Rethinking On-Policy Self-Distillation for Thinking Models** — arXiv:2607.05184 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Analyzes L = sum_t D(pi_T(.|x,c,prefix), pi_S(.|x,prefix)) (JSD, one epoch). Finds fork suppression: the privileged teacher assigns negative advantage to high-entropy exploration markers (wait, hmm, but), EVEN on trajectories that end correct, so thinking models lose 0.8-6.4 pts avg@16 at long budgets while instruct models gain slightly. Dense demonstrations hurt more than answer-only context. No verifier, no fix proposed.
  - relevance: Direct evidence for our M4 observation that the privileged teacher disagrees with correct trajectories, and that the disagreement is style/exploration, not math. Qwen3-4B included. They did not condition on verifier outcome or localize the failure region.
- **Why Does Self-Distillation (Sometimes) Degrade the Reasoning Capability of LLMs?** — arXiv:2603.24472 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Studies privileged-context self-distillation and finds the failure mechanism is suppression of epistemic verbalization: conditioning the teacher on rich context (full solution) makes it overconfident, the student shortens and drops uncertainty expressions, gaining in-domain but losing up to 40 percent OOD. No verifier gating; diagnosis paper.
  - relevance: Second independent confirmation that a gold-solution-conditioned self-teacher pushes on non-math tokens. Warns that any objective built on g'_t will compress exploration unless gated.
- **The Role of Feedback Alignment in Self-Distillation** — arXiv:2606.11173 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Compares self-distillation teachers conditioned on (a) binary reward, (b) a reference solution, (c) step-aligned critiques of the solver's own reasoning. Reference-solution conditioning pressures the student to change behavior at every token, including correct steps, because alternative derivations differ stylistically; step-aligned critique targets only failing tokens and beats GRPO by 16.1 and reference conditioning by 5.3 points.
  - relevance: Names exactly our M4 pathology (successes' disagreement sits in the first third because their approach differs from gold). Their fix uses a generated critique; we are self-contained, but the failure-localized negative mass of g' on failures plays the same role.
- **RLCSD: Reinforcement Learning with Contrastive On-Policy Self-Distillation** — arXiv:2606.11709 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Diagnoses privilege-induced style drift in OPSD. Per-token signal is the teacher-student gap under a CORRECT hint minus the gap under a WRONG hint, cancelling style shifts induced by any hint and concentrating credit on task-bearing tokens; combined with RL. Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B and Olmo-3-7B-Think, beats GRPO and OPSD on math/logic.
  - relevance: Published answer to our M1/M4 style-token problem via a contrast along the hint axis. Our M3 contrast was along the success/failure axis and failed; this axis is untested in our setting.
- **From Generic Correlation to Input-Specific Credit in On-Policy Self-Distillation (CREDIT)** — arXiv:2605.11613 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Base token reward r_t = log pi_ref(y_t|x,prefix,z) - log pi_theta(y_t|x,prefix). Subtracts a batch-contrastive baseline: the same response and feedback z scored with an unrelated input x' in place of x, R_t = r_t - lambda * log pi_ref(y_t|x',prefix,z), lambda=0.1, C=1 extra pass. Suppresses input-generic tokens (imports, filler, templates) and reinforces problem-specific ones. Teacher context may include a successful sibling rollout. Qwen3-8B LCB 49.8 vs SDPO 48.1.
  - relevance: Closest published version of the idea of baselining the privileged contrast to strip format/style mass. Their baseline is across questions; ours (M3) was across outcomes. Neither handles verified-correct off-policy samples.
- **Reward-Gated On-Policy Distillation (RG-OPD)** — arXiv:2607.04037 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Loss = sum_i g_i sum_t m_t KL(pi_theta || pi_T) normalized, with a SEQUENCE-level gate g_i: for verifier-correct trajectories distill only if teacher sequence likelihood exceeds the student's; for incorrect ones only if teacher likelihood is lower; optional margin delta. Top-K=50 reverse KL, frozen external teacher. +2.9 over reverse-KL baseline at 1k tokens.
  - relevance: Explicitly the rule for 'teacher disagrees with a verified-correct trajectory': drop it from distillation. Our M4 says the privileged teacher disagrees with most correct rescued samples, so this gate would discard most of our cliff data; external teacher, not self.
- **SG-OPD: Sign-Consistency Gated On-Policy Distillation** — arXiv:2606.09304 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Uses a binary verifier as a trust signal at two granularities: phased teacher sampling (verifier-endorsed teacher demos early) and token-level sign-consistency gating, applying the full distillation update only where the teacher-student direction agrees with the verifier outcome and shrinking it otherwise. Math benchmarks, +1.98 per-sample, +7.50 per-question over OPD.
  - relevance: Token-level version of the verifier-vs-teacher conflict rule. Our M4 suggests sign consistency would keep only the small positive-g' mass on successes, which barely discriminates.
- **OPPO: Bayesian Value Recursion for Token-Level Credit Assignment** — arXiv:2605.21851 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Log-odds of success updated per token: l_{t+1} = l_t + log lambda_t with log lambda_t = log pi(y_t|x,prefix,y*) - log pi(y_t|x,prefix) (self-oracle = student itself, or a frozen teacher-oracle). Token advantage = change in posterior; direction anchoring forces the sign to match the verifier outcome, so the privileged ratio sets only magnitude. Qwen3-4B: +5.2 AIME24 over GRPO, gains grow with length.
  - relevance: Exactly our M4 quantity, used with the verifier owning the sign and the cumulative privileged ratio owning per-token magnitude. On-policy GRPO; not applied to off-policy rescued samples or inside SFT.
- **Self-Distilled RLVR (RLSD)** — arXiv:2604.03128 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Combines RLVR and privileged self-distillation: verifier correctness gives the update direction, the token-level teacher-student policy difference gives the update magnitude. Motivated by information leakage and instability when the privileged teacher alone supplies the signal. Work in progress.
  - relevance: Same direction-from-verifier, magnitude-from-g' split as OPPO. Our M2/M4 measurements question whether magnitude from g (adapter) carries information; g' magnitude does on failures.
- **Respecting Self-Uncertainty in On-Policy Self-Distillation (EGRSD)** — arXiv:2605.13255 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Token update = reward-grounded direction x teacher-student likelihood-ratio magnitude x entropy gate that down-weights high-teacher-entropy positions with a nonzero floor; CL-EGRSD adds causal lookahead to distinguish sustained from transient high entropy. Qwen3-4B/8B thinking mode; improves the accuracy-length frontier.
  - relevance: Another verifier-sign x privileged-magnitude scheme, with an entropy gate; our M7 Jaccard numbers suggest entropy and g' partly overlap. On-policy only.
- **SCOPE: Signal-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation Enhancement** — arXiv:2604.10688 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Routes rollouts by verifier correctness: incorrect trajectories get teacher-perplexity-weighted KL distillation; CORRECT trajectories get student-perplexity-weighted MLE (plain NLL weighted toward low-confidence tokens at the capability boundary), with group-level normalization. +11.4 percent relative Avg@32 over baselines.
  - relevance: Published form of 'on correct samples do not distill, do surprisal-weighted SFT', i.e. our class-B tokens and C(y) learnability weighting. Their correct samples are on-policy, ours are adapter-rescued.
- **Localizing Credit at the Divergence: Path-Conditioned Self-Distillation (HSD)** — arXiv:2606.15576 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Reverse-KL self-distillation on FAILED rollouts where the teacher is conditioned on the gold answer plus a verified successful sibling rollout from the same group (answer-only fallback). Credit concentrates at the first divergence between the failure and the success because the teacher signal is near baseline on the shared prefix and decays after. Qwen3-8B: +6.9 AIME24, +6.5 AIME25 over GRPO.
  - relevance: Uses sibling successes as privileged context and localizes credit at the divergence without any prefix cut. In our setting the rescued successes could play the sibling role for the 20 rescued failures per question; that combination is unpublished but the objective is theirs.
- **Self-Supervised On-Policy Distillation (SSOPD)** — arXiv:2605.17497 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: L = L_GRPO + lambda_x L_SSOPD; at prefixes of the longest wrong completion, a stop-gradient teacher sees the same prefix plus the shortest correct sibling completion and provides a next-token distribution; the student is distilled toward it. Binary verifier only; no per-token weighting. Qwen3-8B 65.6 macro Avg@12, +1.6 over GRPO, +0.8 over solution-conditioned OPSD.
  - relevance: Shows a sibling-success teacher beats a gold-solution teacher. Teaches only failures; correct rollouts learned by GRPO. Prefix-localized but not prefix-cut.
- **On-Policy Self-Distillation with Sampled Demonstrations** — arXiv:2606.26091 (2026) ✓

Matches RL on average accuracy but sharply reduces functional/semantic diversity and flattens pass@k; fails OOD.
  - relevance: Theory for why demonstration-conditioned self-teachers reward 'looks like the demo' rather than 'is correct', consistent with our M4 first-third disagreement on successes. Warns that weighting rescued successes by g' would collapse diversity.
- **Denser is not Better: Limits of On-Policy Self-Distillation for Continual Post-Training** — arXiv:2607.01763 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Empirical study of SDPO-style dense self-distillation vs GRPO in continual post-training: denser signal causes larger parameter and response drift, amplifies high-frequency formatting artifacts through a self-reinforcing teacher-student loop, generalizes worse OOD and forgets more.
  - relevance: Supports our M1/M3 finding that dense self-teacher signals load onto format tokens; cautions against a dense cliff-specific objective inside an accumulating STaR loop.
- **OGLS-SD: On-Policy Self-Distillation with Outcome-Guided Logit Steering** — arXiv:2605.12400 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Self-reflected teacher logits are recalibrated by contrasting logits from verifier-successful vs failed trajectories to build an outcome-discriminative steering direction applied token-wise, to remove reflection-induced template bias. Math benchmarks; more stable than vanilla OPSD.
  - relevance: Published form of our M3 idea (use the success-vs-failure contrast to purify the teacher signal), done in logit space rather than as a trajectory scalar.
- **Skill-Conditioned Gated Self-Distillation (SGSD)** — arXiv:2605.28791 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: L = sum_k alpha_k rho_k gated_loss_k: teachers conditioned on skill/mistake hints; polarity rho_k in {+1,0,-1} set by comparing the teacher's support sign with verifier success/failure (helpful if it supports a success or suppresses a failure, reversed if opposed, ignored if uncertain); per-token weight uses a bounded gate concentrating on medium gaps. Qwen3-1.7B 37.4 to 43.7 on AIME/HMMT.
  - relevance: Verifier-polarity gating of a privileged self-teacher at the question level; does not address off-policy samples or failure localization.
- **Tailoring Teaching to Aptitude: Direction-Adaptive Self-Distillation (DASD)** — arXiv:2605.22263 (2026) ✓
  - mechanism: Entropy-routed direction: high-entropy tokens are pushed AWAY from the privileged teacher to preserve exploration, low-entropy tokens pulled toward it to stabilize execution. Best macro Avg@16 across six math benchmarks vs RLVR and OPSD.
  - relevance: Treats the privileged teacher as untrustworthy at forks; complementary to our M4 location finding but no verifier conditioning.
- **Geometric Self-Distillation for Reasoning Generalization (GeoSD)** — arXiv:2607.06855 (2026) ✓

Teacher = same model with hints or full solution. +5.7 to +8.6 OOD over baseline at 1.7B-32B.
  - relevance: Soft, student-support-aware version of 'do not follow the teacher where it disagrees'. No verifier.
- **DemoPSD: Disagreement-Modulated Policy Self-Distillation** — arXiv:2607.02502 (2026) ✓

Beats GRPO and SDPO on SciKnowEval, generalizes to GPQA.
  - relevance: Per-token disagreement used to DAMP the privileged signal, not as credit; no verifier.
- **When Are Teacher Tokens Reliable? Position-Weighted OPSD** — arXiv:2605.21606 (2026) ✓

PW-OPSD weights clipped forward-KL targets increasingly toward later positions. +1.0/+1.1 on AIME24/25.
  - relevance: Direct evidence that early disagreement from an answer-conditioned teacher is unreliable and late disagreement is reliable, matching our M4 location split (successes differ early, failures err late).
- **TIP: Token Importance in On-Policy Distillation** — arXiv:2604.14084 (2026) ✓
